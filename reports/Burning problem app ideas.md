# Trucker English prep beats every other bet

The problem to target is commercial drivers losing their right to work over English: build a roadside English and CDL permit prep app with Amharic and Tigrinya explanations, then localise it. It is the only candidate in the notes that combines a livelihood at stake, a fresh enforcement regime (more than 26,000 drivers placed out of service since June 2025, about 2,000 a month), a proven $30 price point in a neighbouring licence niche, and competitors with 0 to 7 ratings. Citizenship test prep with native-language explanations ranks second and Washington driver knowledge test prep third; all three run on one engine (English question, native-language explanation, audio, spoken mock), so the first build is not wasted if the first audience is wrong. Honest expectations stay low: the likely outcome for the top pick is $50 to $400 a month after a year, with roughly a one in ten chance of a sustained $1,000 a month, and that chance depends on localising beyond Habesha languages and on the owner personally reaching driver groups. No note contains direct evidence that Habesha drivers will pay; every verdict rests on price points and ratings counts of adjacent apps, not on reported revenue. Deactivation appeal help, the Washington pay check, birr alerts and "explain this letter" survive only as free features; immigration forms, mileage and tax tools, equb, iddir and trade invoicing are ruled out as paid products for this owner. The first build should ship free and complete, go to drivers through Telegram, Facebook and WhatsApp groups, and only then put the full content behind a one-time unlock near $19.99.

## Paying to keep a job beats paying for convenience

The base rates have not moved since the earlier round and they frame everything below. The median subscription app earns about **$72 a month** one year after launch, 17.3% reach $1,000 a month within two years, and 4.6% reach $10,000; that sample already excludes apps that never integrated a payments SDK ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). New subscription app launches rose from about 2,000 a month in January 2022 to 14,700 a month in January 2026, and apps launched in 2025 or later hold 3% of subscription revenue ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). An AI coding agent removes build cost for this owner and for everyone else, so the choice of problem has to supply what the agent cannot: urgency strong enough that people look for the tool, and an audience the incumbents do not reach.

The one cross-niche dataset in the notes ranks problems by the share of apps earning at least $1,000 a month. It is a third-party estimate limited to apps that reached a top chart, so it flatters every category, but the ordering is informative: trade invoice and estimate makers 39%, exam prep for one licence or certification 18%, personal finance micro-tools 19%, faith apps 19%, caregiver and medication coordination 11% ([Trend Apps](https://trendapps.dev/blog/trends/profitable-mobile-app-ideas-2026/)). Tools the buyer earns money with convert best, tools the buyer must pass or comply with come second, and tools for people who are worried about someone else come last. The owner's instinct that the church companion was "not burning enough" is half supported: faith sits level with exam prep in that table, so the real difference is not category but whether a deadline and an income are attached.

Two pricing facts shape the recommendation. First, a subscription for a one-off need leaks out by design: one-year retention is 44.1% on yearly plans, 17.0% on monthly and 3.4% on weekly, and nearly 30% of annual subscriptions are cancelled in the first month ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). Someone who studies for a test and passes has no reason to keep paying, so a one-time unlock priced against the stakes is the honest model, and it has precedent: TurboScan Pro is estimated at about $10,000 a month as a $14.99 one-time purchase ([Trend Apps](https://trendapps.dev/app/ios/342548956/)). Second, a hard paywall converts a median 10.7% of downloads against 2.1% for freemium ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)). The owner's rule (a working app given free, then a paywall) sits between the two: it sacrifices early revenue to buy trust and proof, then moves toward the hard-paywall end once people have seen the thing work. That is a sound trade in a community that does not join waitlists or pledge, and it replaces the pledge-button test from the earlier round entirely.

The evidence has a hole that no amount of synthesis fills. The researchers found **no documented solo success from 2024 to 2026 that publishes revenue in a strictly urgent niche** such as exam prep, compliance or trades; the named solo wins (a photo vault at a self-reported $41,000 in one month, a habit tracker at $15,000 to $28,000 a month) are utilities that won on App Store search, and all are founder-reported ([Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/41k-from-a-boring-appwithout-ads), [Starter Story mirror](https://rosetta.to/u/starterstory/how-i-built-it-15k-month-mobile-app)). No revenue figure was retrieved for any competitor named in this report. "People pay" below means "apps charge this price and have this many ratings", which is weaker than it sounds.

### Rank 1: an enforcement shock with almost nobody serving it

English proficiency returned to the roadside out-of-service criteria on 2025-06-25. The check has two parts, conversational English with the inspector and reading English highway signs ([Transport Topics](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/english-proficiency-rule-2026), [Truckinginfo](https://www.truckinginfo.com/articles/when-language-stops-the-truck-the-return-of-elp-out-of-service-orders)). The Department of Transportation says **more than 26,000 commercial drivers** have been placed out of service since then, and a trucking association executive put the pace at about 2,000 a month; in 2024 the count was zero ([FreightWaves](https://freightwaves.com/news/after-sidelining-26000-truck-drivers-fmcsa-seeks-to-lock-in-english-rules), [Truckinginfo](https://www.truckinginfo.com/10241165/transportation-secretary-promises-to-go-after-bad-actors-in-trucking)). The testing side is tightening in the same direction: Texas made all CDL knowledge exams English-only with interpreters prohibited from 2026-06-01, and the Transportation Secretary announced in February 2026 that all truck and bus drivers will take CDL tests in English ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/news/dps-announces-changes-cdl-knowledge-testing), [Anchorage Daily News](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/02/20/all-truckers-and-bus-drivers-will-be-required-to-take-commercial-drivers-license-tests-in-english/)). A driver who used to pass in his own language, or drive with limited English, now needs bilingual preparation: the English question and answer, explained in the language he thinks in.

Supply has not caught up. The dedicated trucker-English apps are weeks old and invisible: TruckSpeak has 0 ratings, Trucker English 1, Lingo Truck 7 ([TruckSpeak](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6822367936), [Trucker English](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6807656745), [Lingo Truck](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6756713641)). The large CDL prep apps (48,906 ratings for the leader) are English and Spanish ([CDL Test Prep](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1527903479)). The closest model is **CDL Help, 5,413 ratings at 4.87 stars, localised into Arabic, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Chinese, Spanish, Turkish, Ukrainian and Uzbek**, which proves immigrant-language CDL prep works and leaves out Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali and Punjabi ([CDL Help](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6444388755)). The price evidence comes from a sibling niche: TLC Practice Exam, the New York taxi and for-hire licence exam app, sells at **$29.99 paid upfront with 2,687 ratings** and multilingual study support ([TLC Practice Exam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6447449111)). Drivers also pay $10.99 to $18.99 a month for tools that protect income ([Mystro](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mystro-driver-drive-deliver/id1524407919), [Everlance](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mileage-tracker-by-everlance/id985378916)).

The weaknesses are real. **Nobody has measured how many Ethiopian, Eritrean or Somali truckers exist** or how many of the 26,000 speak those languages; trade coverage focuses on Spanish and Punjabi speakers. An app cannot help the separate group losing non-domiciled CDLs under the February 2026 rule, which the regulator expects to remove up to 194,000 holders over five years ([FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/newsroom/trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-puts-safety-first-finalizes-rule-stop)). No app makes a person conversational in a month; the product is rehearsal of one specific encounter and of sign vocabulary, and the listing must not promise more. The demand is also a creature of policy. The regulator proposed on 2026-08-10 to codify English proficiency as an out-of-service violation, which makes the rule stickier through 2027, but a court or a later administration could deflate the segment ([J. J. Keller](https://jjkellercompliancenetwork.com/regsense/91-fr-51422-english-language-proficiency-out-of-service-criteria)). The hedge is the engine: the same code carries the next two modules, whose demand does not depend on this rule. One practical advantage is worth naming as reasoning, not as a sourced fact: the roadside check is a federal procedure, so one content set serves the whole country, while ordinary licence tests need state-by-state upkeep.

### Rank 2: citizenship prep has the verified gap and a capped price

The need is large and has sharpened. About 818,500 people naturalised in fiscal 2024 ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-09-16_IF12322_42b1a898aecd778ae52b06eab04bcc9fc2d453d7.html)). Applications filed from 2025-10-20 face a **128-question bank, up to 20 questions asked and 12 correct to pass**, double the old thresholds ([Boundless](https://boundless.com/blog/us-announces-major-changes-to-naturalization-civics-test)). One policy brief reports the application denial rate rising from 8.3% to 18.1% between May 2024 and May 2026; that figure came from a search summary and conflicts in level with law-firm tallies of about 89% approval, so treat the direction as credible and the size as unconfirmed ([NFAP](https://nfap.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Naturalization-Trends.NFAP-Policy-Brief.July-2026.pdf), [Manifest Law](https://manifestlaw.com/blog/n400-approval-rate)). The filing fee is $760 on paper or $710 online, and a June 2026 proposal would raise it to $1,330 and $1,280 ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/why-fewer-green-card-holders-could-become-us-citizens-12175364)). A failed interview costs months and possibly a second fee.

The English shelf is full: one app has 104,311 ratings, another 65,882 with an AI interview simulator at **$3.99 a month or $19.99 one-time**, and the best multilingual app, Citizenry, covers 20 languages with spoken mock interviews ([US Citizenship Test 2026 Plus](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1103037173), [Citizen Now](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1492938869), [AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1492938869), [Citizenry](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6451455299)). None lists Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali or Dari, and App Store searches for citizenship tests in Amharic and Tigrinya return only generic English apps ([iTunes Search, Amharic](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=citizenship+test+amharic&entity=software&country=us), [iTunes Search, Tigrinya](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=citizenship+test+tigrinya&entity=software&country=us)). The test is taken in English, so the product is "learn the English answers with explanations in your language"; applicants who qualify for the age and residency exemptions take civics in their own language and need native content outright, though nobody counted them.

Three cautions keep this in second place. The price is capped near $20 by strong incumbents. The Habesha volume is small: about 304,000 US residents were born in Ethiopia and 45,000 in Eritrea, and neither country is in the top ten for naturalisations ([MPI](https://migrationpolicy.org/article/sub-saharan-african-immigrants-united-states)). And the nearest precedents are discouraging: an Arabic citizenship app at $12.99 has 5 ratings and no update since 2022, and a creator-led Arabic, English and Spanish app has 70 ratings after 15 months ([Arabic US Citizenship Test](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1571047429), [Ameer Ali](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6749251462)). A language gap is not the same as a paying market. Against that, this is the cheapest build of all (a fixed national question bank with free official study materials), the audience is settled residents the owner can reach through Seattle churches and community associations, and the demand does not vanish if the 2025 test is revised.

### Rank 3: the Washington knowledge test is the durable piece

Washington offers the driver knowledge test in 12 languages, none of them Amharic, Tigrinya or Somali, with an online practice test in English and Spanish only; yet the same agency publishes its website in all three languages, which concedes the communities are large enough to serve ([WA DOL](https://dol.wa.gov/node/254), [WA DOL Amharic site](https://dol.wa.gov/am/node/184)). DC, Maryland and Virginia offer the test in Amharic, and no state was found offering Tigrinya ([DC DMV](https://dmv.dc.gov/release/district-dmv-offers-driver-knowledge-test-15-languages), [Maryland MVA](https://mva.maryland.gov/news/maryland-learners-permit-exam-now-available-17-languages-including-american-sign-language), [Virginia DMV](https://transactions-t.dmv.virginia.gov/drivers/know_lang.asp)). The broadest big app covers English, Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese and Hindi ([myDMV](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1625234008)). The need is permanent and sits in the owner's home market, which is why it ranks at all. It ranks third because the buyers are new arrivals with little money and free alternatives, the realistic price is $7.99 to $14.99, content is state-specific and must be kept current, and a Washington driving school already advertises the test in Amharic with a translator, a claim the state page does not confirm ([BeSafe Drivers Ed](https://sites.google.com/besafedriversed.org/besafe-drivers-ed/services/knowledge-test)). Its best role is the free, trust-building module of the trucker app, tested face to face in Seattle.

### Rank 4: CNA and home care aide prep waits its turn

Competition is thin (the leading CNA apps have 2,265 and 1,958 ratings, English only) and Washington has documented the language barrier: the Somali-language pass rate on the home care aide written exam was 78.9% in 2017 and 56.6% in 2018, and the State Auditor named language as a certification barrier ([Easy Pass CNA](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502053307), [Crosscut](https://uat1.crosscut.com/2018/10/would-be-home-health-workers-claim-bias-states-qualifying-exam), [WA State Auditor](https://www.sao.wa.gov/sites/default/files/audit_reports/ar1018059.pdf)). Those pass rates are eight years old, nobody confirmed whether the exam is offered in Amharic or Tigrinya today, and no count of test-takers was found. It is a credible fourth module for the same engine, not a first product. In the same family, NCLEX prep for foreign-trained nurses has the starkest failure rate (about 47% first-time pass against about 87% for US graduates) but belongs to entrenched incumbents and demands clinical content no solo owner can vouch for; food handler cards are too cheap to matter; security guard prep is an empty shelf with no demand evidence ([NCSBN](https://ncsbn.org/public-files/2025_NCLEXExamStats_Final.pdf)).

## Nine candidates fail as paid products

**Rideshare deactivation appeal help** is the hardest to set aside because it is the most painful event in the gig notes. Chicago data show 30,881 drivers deactivated since 2019 and 1,085 reinstated, about 3.5%, and appeals run through the app in English ([The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2026/09/23/rideshare-drivers-want-fair-hearing-before-losing-their-jobs/)). It fails on three counts. In Washington the fix is already free: the state-contracted Driver Resource Center, run by Drivers Union, represents drivers in appeals, and a university study found about 80% of drivers it assisted were reactivated ([WA L&I](https://lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/industry-specific-requirements/transportation-network-company-drivers-rights/resource-center-and-deactivations), [The Columbian](https://www.columbian.com/news/2023/aug/07/uw-study-finds-racial-bias-in-rideshare-driver-deactivations/)). The gap is in other states, where the owner has no presence. The customer has just lost his income, the worst moment to ask for money, and nobody pays an app for this today. And drafting an appeal drifts toward legal advice. A free in-language checklist ("what triggers deactivation, who to call") is worth including in a driver app; a paid product is not.

**The Washington driver pay and tax check** is the most original idea in the notes and the least proven. Since 2026-07-01 each rideshare company must give drivers a downloadable record of per-trip data for the previous 24 months, and the 2026 minimums are published ($0.70 per passenger minute and $1.63 per mile in Seattle), so an app could check the driver's own export against the legal floor without touching Uber or Lyft credentials ([WA L&I](https://www.lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/industry-specific-requirements/transportation-network-company-drivers-rights/getting-paid)). That matters because credential-based tools are exactly what Lyft has threatened deactivation over ([The Rideshare Guy](https://therideshareguy.com/weekly-roundup-lyft-threatens-driver-deactivation-over-third-party-earnings-apps/)). But no note shows that underpayment actually occurs, what the export looks like, or that a driver would pay to find out. If the companies comply, the app's answer is "you were paid correctly" and the value is gone. A tip-deduction estimate is a natural companion (eligible gig workers can deduct up to $25,000 of tips for tax years 2025 to 2028) but the notes could not establish whether ITIN holders qualify, and anything that tells a user what to claim reads as tax advice ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-working-families-tax-cuts-what-gig-economy-workers-should-know)). Treat both as a weekend prototype after the first app ships, and as reel material for Habesha AI now.

**Birr rate alerts** solve a visible problem: on one day licensed apps quoted from about 161 to 181 birr per dollar for the same $100 transfer, a spread worth roughly $20 on $200 ([Monito](https://www.monito.com/send-money/united-states/ethiopia/usd/etb)). That reading rests on a single fetch. The business does not exist: comparison is already free, an indie birr-rate app has 0 ratings, affiliate payouts were listed as low as $1.60 per first transfer, and most senders already hold accounts with the providers ([ET Forex](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/et-forex-birr-exchange-rates/id6811115038), [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/remitly-affiliate-program)). Showing parallel-market rates, the number people actually want, invites trouble given the National Bank of Ethiopia's warnings against unlicensed channels ([NBE](https://nbe.gov.et/nbe_news/press-release-notice-on-the-use-of-licensed-money-transfer-agents-for-remittances-to-ethiopia/)). At most it is a free widget that keeps an app on the home screen.

**The iddir dues tracker** has an empty shelf and the same flaw the owner already named in the church app: nothing is on fire. No purpose-built tool was found, but no data exists on how many US iddirs there are, how they keep records, or whether a treasurer would pay ([Cheddar Up](https://www.cheddarup.com/membership-management-software)). **Equb** is worse: seven or more tracker apps sit at 0 to 200 ratings, the real pain (a member who defaults) is only solved by holding money, and holding money means money-transmitter licensing state by state ([Junta](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/junta-trusted-savings/id1530022030), [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/administrative-rulings/application-money-services-business-2)).

**Explain-this-letter** was shipped by at least five solo developers in 2026 and none has more than 33 US ratings; people already point a general AI assistant or a translator camera at the page ([BriefAI](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757674090), [ExplainThisDoc](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6758530963), [WhatSays](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6446993803)). It also carries the highest liability of the set, since a wrong deadline on an immigration or court notice does real harm, and sending a photographed letter to a cloud model requires explicit consent under Apple's rule on sharing personal data with third-party AI ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ey6d8onl)). **Immigration forms and case tracking** combine funded incumbents (three trackers above 170,000 ratings each) with the heaviest unauthorised-practice-of-law exposure; the diversity visa lottery angle is seasonal, aimed at people abroad, and its helper apps have 0 to 14 ratings ([Case Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id921827126), [Lawfully](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1435063223), [DV Photo Tool](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6756299301)).

**Mileage and tax tools** are solved for free. Stride has 99,516 ratings and no paid tier, six more trackers hold 20,000 to 116,000 ratings each, and filing belongs to apps with up to 937,850 ratings ([Stride](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stride-mileage-tax-tracker/id1041591359), [MileIQ](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mileiq-mileage-tracker-log/id578830929), [TurboTax](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/turbotax-file-your-tax-return/id940247939)). Auto-accept tools carry the highest willingness to pay in gig work and would risk getting community members deactivated, which would destroy the one asset the owner has. **Trade invoicing** deserves a fair hearing because it has the best earning rate in the data (39%), prices of $10 to $59 a month, and incumbents with documented outages and price rises ([Trend Apps](https://trendapps.dev/blog/trends/profitable-mobile-app-ideas-2026/), [MicroGaps](https://www.microgaps.com/gaps/solo-contractor-estimate-invoice-app)). It is ruled out for this owner specifically: he has no access to tradespeople, the named winners are companies, business users expect support and reliability from a part-time solo owner, and Android apps in the niche are far more likely to be earning than iOS ones (53% against 31%).

## More languages enlarge the market, iPhone-only shrinks it

### Somali, Oromo, Dari and Punjabi matter more than Arabic

A Habesha-only test prep app has a low ceiling: the largest Habesha-specific app on the US store, an Amharic Bible, has about 9,500 ratings after 14 years ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/holy-bible-in-amharic/id534343507)). The engine's value is that each new language is content, not code. In Seattle's own data the Afro-Asiatic language group splits **Somali 37.7%, Amharic 25.0%, Oromo 21.5%, Tigrinya 16.3%**, so adding Somali and Oromo more than doubles the local audience ([City of Seattle OIRA](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/OIRA/2026%20Commonly%20Spoken%20Languages%20in%20Seattle-FINAL.pdf)). Where each language is missing differs by product. For CDL and roadside English the open languages are Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali and Punjabi, and Punjabi is the largest prize given where press coverage of out-of-service drivers concentrates. For citizenship the open languages are Amharic, Tigrinya, Somali and Dari. For the Washington knowledge test, Arabic and Punjabi are already offered by the state, while Somali, Amharic, Tigrinya and Dari are not. **Arabic is the weakest expansion**: the leading multilingual CDL and citizenship apps already cover it, and the one paid Arabic citizenship app drew 5 ratings. Tigrinya is the emptiest shelf everywhere and the riskiest to produce, because machine output is least reliable there.

### Work tools skew Android and the build is iPhone-only

The owner builds native SwiftUI, and that cuts the audience for work tools. In the category estimates, Android apps are roughly twice as likely as iOS apps to be earning in trade invoicing (53% against 31%) and personal finance tools (30% against 14%), and many gig tools started on Android ([Trend Apps](https://trendapps.dev/blog/trends/profitable-mobile-app-ideas-2026/)). The phone split among Habesha drivers, truckers and new arrivals is unknown, and the earlier round judged Google Play likely larger for this diaspora without measuring it. The counterweight is that iOS takes 84.75% of subscription revenue overall, so iPhone owners are the ones who pay ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)). The practical consequence: "which phone do you use" is the first question in every driver conversation, and if most answers are Android the plan needs a rethink before a build, not after. The split likely favours citizenship (settled residents of five years or more) over the knowledge test (new arrivals).

### The work the agent cannot do

Claude Code can build the question engine, the progress tracking, the spoken mock using on-device English speech recognition, the store listing and the paywall. It cannot supply four things, and they decide the outcome.

Native-speaker review comes first. Every Amharic and Tigrinya explanation must be read by a fluent person before release; a wrong explanation of a traffic rule or an inspector's question is worse than none. The owner can review Amharic himself if he is fluent in writing it, and must recruit a Tigrinya reviewer, and later Somali, Oromo, Dari and Punjabi reviewers, since he cannot judge those at all. Audio is second. Independent tests put Amharic speech recognition at 23% to 50% word error rate and Tigrinya at 35% or worse, and a leading voice vendor's standard models list neither language nor Somali, so explanation audio must be recorded by people ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23654v1), [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech)). Text-only explanations are acceptable in a first release; machine-voiced Tigrinya is not. Third is reach. Habesha AI's reels at a few hundred views deliver a handful of installs each at best. The channel that matters is the one incumbents cannot enter: trucker and driver Telegram, Facebook and WhatsApp groups, Habesha-owned trucking companies and CDL schools, community tax preparers, and for the other modules the Ethiopian and Eritrean community associations and churches. Someone must be let into those groups and vouch for the app in person. Fourth is trust after launch: answering messages in Amharic, fixing a wrong answer the day it is reported, and being honest about what the app can and cannot do at a roadside stop.

## Honest numbers for the top three

These ranges are judgement, not measurement. They are anchored on the $72 median, the 17.3% base rate, the price points above and plausible install counts for a community-distributed app with no ad spend; they assume the Small Business Program rate of 15%, which requires enrolment ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). A $19.99 unlock nets about $17. Because the unlock is one-time, every month's income needs that month's new buyers: $1,000 a month means about 60 new purchases every month, indefinitely.

| App | Likely monthly revenue at 12 months | Good case | Odds of sustained $1,000 a month | What the good case requires |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trucker roadside English and CDL prep, $19.99 unlock | $50 to $400 | $800 to $2,500 | about 10% (5% with Habesha languages only, up to 15% with Somali and Punjabi) | Entry to driver groups, native reviewers for each language, the rule staying in force |
| Citizenship prep with native-language explanations, $9.99 to $14.99 | $40 to $300 | $500 to $1,500 | 5% to 8% | Community associations and churches recommending it, plus Somali and Dari editions |
| Washington driver knowledge test prep, $9.99 | $20 to $130 | $300 to $800 | 2% to 4% | Driving schools and resettlement groups handing it out, more states, more languages |

The arithmetic behind the first row: 1,500 to 5,000 installs in a year would be a strong result for a diaspora app, and a high-intent audience converting at 5% to 10% yields 75 to 500 purchases, or roughly $1,300 to $8,500 for the year. The good case assumes several languages, each with its own groups, and a steady flow of new and worried drivers. All three rows can stack on one app, which is the main reason to build an engine; stacked, a realistic year-one total is still $100 to $600 a month. Two failure modes are more likely than any revenue figure: the owner cannot get into the driver groups, or drivers use the free version, pass around screenshots and never pay. Running costs are near zero, so the downside is time.

## First build: free roadside English app, then a one-time unlock

### Four cheap checks before any code

Run these in one to two weeks; they cost almost nothing and each can kill or redirect the plan.

The first is an App Store keyword and competitor check, using Apple's free search interface, which returns ratings counts, prices and last-update dates for any term ([DEV](https://dev.to/jack_fruit/how-to-check-an-app-idea-against-real-app-store-data-before-you-build-it-4cp0)). Search "CDL English", "trucker English", "ELP test", "CDL Amharic", "CDL Somali", "CDL Punjabi" and the Amharic-script equivalents, and record whether any new app has passed 100 ratings since November 2026. Install the three trucker-English apps and CDL Help and note what they teach and charge. An ASO tool at about $9 a month adds popularity scores; practitioners aim for popularity of roughly 20 to 55 with low difficulty, but those thresholds come from tool vendors and the popularity feed was disrupted between October 2025 and October 2026 ([Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/keyword-difficulty-explained), [Indie App Devs](https://indieappdevs.substack.com/p/indie-app-devs-4)). Expect native-language terms to show little or no volume. That confirms the app will be found through groups, not search, and should not be read as absence of need.

The second is checking the two places the researchers did not look: Google Play and YouTube. If an Android app or a popular Amharic YouTube series already teaches roadside English, the gap is smaller than it appears.

The third, and the one that matters most, is talking to five to ten drivers, at least three of them truckers, found through personal contacts, a Habesha trucking company or a driver group. Ask what phone they use; whether they or someone they know was stopped or put out of service for English; what they did about it and what it cost; where they would look for help; which groups they are in and whether they would share a link there; and whether Tigrinya, Somali or Oromo speakers they know have the same problem. Do not ask whether they would pay; hand them the app later and watch. If the owner cannot find three Habesha truckers in two weeks, he cannot distribute a trucker app, and the first module should be citizenship, which he can test in Seattle in person.

The fourth is a Tigrinya quality test: have the agent draft twenty explanations in Amharic and Tigrinya and give them to native readers. If the Tigrinya needs heavy rewriting, launch in Amharic alone and add Tigrinya when a reviewer has time.

### What to ship and when to charge

Version one is a complete, working, free app with three parts: an inspector conversation drill (the questions an officer asks, with model English answers, the user speaking aloud, and an explanation in Amharic or Tigrinya), highway sign reading with meanings in the native language, and CDL general knowledge practice with native-language explanations. Add the Washington driver knowledge test as a small second section, because it gives non-truckers in Seattle a reason to install and lets the owner test the engine face to face. Keep everything on the device, with no account and no cloud AI at runtime; that avoids per-user cost, the third-party AI consent screen and most privacy work. Make no pass guarantee, state plainly that the app is practice and not legal or regulatory advice, and submit under Education. Apple's rule against apps "indistinguishable from what's already widely available" is a reason to lead the listing with the languages, since that is what makes this app different from fifty CDL quiz clones ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

Give it away for six to eight weeks through the groups found in the driver conversations, with one or two Habesha AI reels as a secondary push. Set the pass line before launch. A reasonable one, offered as judgement: 150 installs, a third of users finishing at least one full drill set, visible sharing inside at least two groups, and a few unprompted messages saying it helped. Under 50 installs after real effort in the groups means the channel does not work, and that answer is worth having for the price of a few weeks.

If it passes, add a one-time unlock at $19.99 (test $14.99 and $29.99 later) for the full question bank, all sign sets and the complete mock inspection, and leave a useful first section and the Washington module free permanently. Let everyone who installed during the free period keep full access; in a community that runs on word of mouth, the first users are the sales force, and charging them would cost more than it earns. Then add languages in order of reviewer availability, Somali first given Seattle's numbers, and build the citizenship module second.

## Conclusion

The owner asked for a burning problem and the research returns a narrower answer than expected: the only fires in these notes that a solo iPhone developer can safely approach are tests, because a test is the one high-stakes moment where the remedy is information, the law permits anyone to sell practice, and the big apps have skipped his languages. Everything hotter (deactivation, immigration status, money transfer) is either served free by an institution, fenced by licensing, or dangerous to get wrong. The earlier conclusion, that distribution decides income, still holds; what changes is the asset being distributed. A church companion asked the community for affection. A roadside English app asks a driver whether he wants to keep his truck moving, and that question either produces purchases or it does not.

The plan's weakest joint is not the product or the policy but an unmeasured assumption: that enough Habesha and Somali truckers exist, carry iPhones, and sit in groups the owner can enter. Ten conversations will reveal more about that than any further desk research, and they should happen before a line of code is written. If they go badly, the same engine pointed at the citizenship test is a slower, smaller and safer version of the same idea, and it is the honest fallback.
