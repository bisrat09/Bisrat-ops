# Distribution, not code, decides app income

An AI coding agent can build and ship almost any small native iOS app for this owner, but the realistic 12-month income from one such app is **tens to low hundreds of dollars a month**, and the honest odds of a sustained $1,000 a month are under 10% for every idea examined here. The best-measured benchmark puts the median subscription app at about **$72 a month one year after launch**, with only 17.3% reaching $1,000 a month within two years, and that sample already excludes apps that never earned anything ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)). New App Store supply roughly doubled in 2026 while downloads grew about 2%, so building became free and attention became the scarce input ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026-07-20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/)). The owner's only real edge is the one thing Claude Code cannot produce: trust and access inside the Ethiopian and Eritrean diaspora, where existing apps are fragmented and mostly tiny. The best bet is therefore a bilingual Orthodox Tewahedo daily companion (fasting calendar, Senksar, prayers, mezmur lyrics), with a body-fat goal tracker carved out of Fitlog as the second option. Selling Fitlog as a Fitbod competitor is the weakest of the plausible paths. Apple does not penalize AI-written code, running costs are near zero, and the correct first move is a three-week demand test through Seattle parishes and a TestFlight thin slice before any full build. Treat any of these as a low-cost learning project with a small chance of a modest side income, not as a revenue plan.

## The median subscription app earns $72 a month

RevenueCat's 2026 report covers 115,000+ apps and $16B+ in revenue, and it describes a market with a tiny head and a long near-zero tail. One year after launch, the median app earns about $72 a month, the top quartile $429, and the top 10% above $2,574. **Only 17.3% reach $1,000 a month within two years and 4.6% reach $10,000**, and both shares fell from the prior year (19% and 5.3%) ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). Adapty's separate dataset of 16,000 apps agrees on the shape: the top 10% of apps capture 94.5% of subscription revenue, and **57.7% of new subscription apps earn under $1,000 in total** ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)). Both datasets only include apps that integrated a paid-subscription SDK, so the true median for a quietly published solo app sits lower. A hackathon population gives a feel for the floor: of 3,336 projects in RevenueCat's Shipaton 2026, 88.6% solo-built, about 1% disclosed any revenue at all ([PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/shipaton-2026-data)).

The unit economics explain why. Median download-to-paid conversion is 2.0% by day 35 (2.6% to 2.8% in North America), and realized first-year value per paying user is about $32 in North America ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)). A thousand US downloads are therefore worth roughly $830 over a year at the median, and $1,000 a month needs something like 1,200 to 1,500 quality downloads every month, sustained. That calculation is the whole problem in one line: revenue is a download-volume question, and nothing about AI-assisted building changes download volume.

### What earns and what fails

Category data favors narrow, high-intent products. Health & Fitness has the highest download-to-paid rate (2.9%) and the highest first-year value per payer, though it also has the lowest first-renewal retention at 30.3%. Utilities retain best: 58.1% of trial subscribers renew after the first cycle and trials lift lifetime value by 85.1% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)). The crowded end is easy to identify. Among 2,139 published Shipaton apps, Health & Fitness was the largest category, and the biggest keyword clusters were focus blockers (about 105), **workout trackers (about 100)**, journals, sleep apps, habit trackers and budgeting apps, with 229 apps carrying "AI" in the name ([PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/shipaton-2026-data)). AI features cut both ways: AI apps earn 41% more per payer and churn 30% faster ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/)).

On pricing, the evidence supports a free download with an annual plan plus a lifetime or one-time unlock. Paid-upfront is nearly extinct (7 of the Shipaton apps charge up front), one-time purchases inside subscription apps are growing (in Lifestyle, from 5.9% of revenue in 2023 to 26.3% in 2025), and weekly plans, which now produce 55.5% of subscription revenue, are a paid-acquisition tactic with about 1% of subscribers left after a year ([PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/shipaton-2026-data), [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)). Hard paywalls convert about five times better than freemium with near-identical one-year retention, although apps that choose hard paywalls tend to have high-intent traffic already. Ads are not a serious option at solo scale, and for any app touching HealthKit they are closed off by rule.

Individual stories confirm that distribution, not product, separates outcomes. HabitKit's solo developer reported $602,000 in 2025 revenue and credits a top-five US ranking for "habit tracker", a YouTube feature and 20,000 followers. His second app, a polished native SwiftUI build launched to that same audience, made **$874 in its first month and $52 in monthly recurring revenue** ([Sebastian Röhl](https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2025-the-year-that-changed-everything)). Loggd, a habit app, reached about €250 a month after eight months, with more than 70% of users coming from daily Threads posts ([Loggd](https://loggd.life/blog/build-in-public-habit-tracker-saturated-niche)). The two AI-built apps with public numbers earned about $500 and $3,000 in profit, in line with the general median and not above it ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026-07-20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/)). All of these are self-reported and survivorship-biased upward.

## Three thousand new apps a day crowd every free channel

Sensor Tower counted about 600,000 new App Store apps in 2025 and about **560,000 in the first half of 2026 alone**, roughly 3,000 a day, while downloads rose 2% ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026-07-20/report-app-store-added-nearly-as-many-new-apps-in-h1-2026-as-in-all-of-2025/)). Search remains the main discovery surface: Apple says nearly 65% of downloads happen directly after a search ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/18/app-store-search-results-more-ads-2026/)). For a new app with no ratings, that means long-tail keywords, and long-tail keywords are small. Apple's popularity index bottoms out at 5, meaning "little to no searches at all", and many niche terms sit there ([Appfigures](https://help.appfigures.com/en/article/understanding-app-store-optimization-metrics-1pcnt7t/)). No reliable public table converts keyword popularity into downloads, so the working assumption for a culture-specific app is single-digit daily downloads from search: easy to rank for "Tewahedo" or "Tigrinya", little volume when you do. Two free tools fit a niche app well. Apple now reads screenshot text and metadata with AI to assign tags, which rewards plain-language captions, and custom product pages can be tied to specific keywords ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/14/the-app-stores-new-ai-generated-tags-are-live-in-the-beta), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/apple-unveils-major-app-store-connect-updates-at-wwdc-2025/)).

Habesha AI's reels are not an install channel at their current reach. No independent dataset measures view-to-install conversion for organic short video, and nearly everything published comes from vendors. One of those vendors concedes that "a video can hit 500K views and generate 40 installs if it reaches the wrong audience" ([TokPortal](https://www.tokportal.com/verticals/tiktok-organic-distribution-mobile-apps-games)). Applying the range implied by the available examples to a reel with 300 views yields between zero and one or two installs, so three reels a week produce perhaps 0 to 20 installs a month. The brand's value is fit, not size: a diaspora app shown to a diaspora audience converts at the upper end of that range, and a general fitness app shown to the same audience is the wrong-audience case.

The other free channels are thinly documented and mostly small. Product Hunt and Reddit launches by solo developers typically yield tens of downloads, and Product Hunt's audience has nothing to do with a diaspora consumer app. Apple featuring has a real, free process: a Featuring Nomination in App Store Connect with at least two weeks' notice, judged on criteria that include uniqueness and "culturally relevant and appropriate content" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)). A well-made Amharic or Tigrinya app nominated ahead of Ethiopian New Year fits that brief better than another workout tracker does, though Apple publishes no acceptance rate. No data exists on churches or community organizations as app distribution channels. That absence is the point: they are high-trust, concentrated and free of algorithms, and they are the one channel where this owner starts ahead of the template developers.

Paid search does not rescue a low-priced app. US median cost per install in 2025 was $3.77 in Health & Fitness, $2.62 in Lifestyle, $2.91 in Education and $2.09 in Reference ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks?lang=en-US)). At a 2% to 2.6% paid conversion rate, a paying customer costs roughly $100 to $190, several times what a $20 to $40 annual plan returns. A $100 to $300 Apple Ads test buys 80 to 200 installs and is worth running only for keyword-level conversion data.

## Apple rejects sameness, not AI authorship

Apple has no rule against apps written with AI tools. Xcode 26.3 ships agentic coding with Claude Agent and Codex built in ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/26/apple-releases-xcode-26-3-with-support-for-agentic-coding/)), and the much-discussed March 2026 "vibe coding crackdown" targeted builder apps such as Replit that generate and run code on the device under guideline 2.5.2, not apps authored with AI ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/apple-reportedly-blocks-vibe-coding-apps-from-publishing-updates/)). A native SwiftUI binary submitted by its owner is outside that enforcement entirely.

The real exposure is ordinary and sits in four places. First, guideline 4.3(b), tightened in June 2026, bars apps "indistinguishable from what's already widely available" and adds that Apple "may remove these apps from the App Store going forward if they are not updated, improved, or do not attract customers" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/09/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines/)). Apple says App Review now uses AI to analyze app similarity ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/the-app-store-stopped-over-2-point-2-billion-usd-in-fraudulent-transactions-in-2025/)). A generic workout tracker is squarely in the risk zone, while a Tewahedo companion is differentiated by nature. Second, guideline 4.1(c), added in November 2025, forbids using another developer's brand or product name in an app's name or icon without approval, which rules out marketing Fitlog as a "Fitbod replacement" in its title ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=ey6d8onl)). Third, privacy: every app needs a privacy policy in the listing and in the app, account deletion if accounts exist, and, since November 2025, explicit permission before personal data goes to "third-party AI" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)). The conservative reading is an in-app consent screen naming the model provider before the first API call. Fourth, health data: HealthKit data cannot be used for advertising, each data type read must be disclosed, apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud", and health-measurement accuracy claims invite extra scrutiny ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). For Fitlog that means re-reading weight and body fat from HealthKit on each device and presenting body fat as data from Health, not as the app's own measurement.

A first-submission rejection is common and usually curable. Apple reviewed over 9.1 million submissions in 2025 and rejected over 2 million, including 371,000+ as spam, copycat or misleading and 443,000+ for privacy violations ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/the-app-store-stopped-over-2-point-2-billion-usd-in-fraudulent-transactions-in-2025/)). No rejection rate for AI-built apps is published. Two areas were not researched and matter for the ideas below: the Kids Category rules for a children's Fidel app, and Washington's My Health My Data Act for a Seattle developer handling body-composition data.

### Costs are small enough to ignore until revenue exists

The Apple Developer account is already paid through September 2027. Under the App Store Small Business Program, Apple takes 15% on sales up to $1M in annual proceeds and 10% on subscriptions after their first year, but enrollment is required and is not automatic, so it belongs before the first sale ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). RevenueCat is free up to $2,500 in monthly tracked revenue ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing/)). A $39.99 annual plan nets about $33.99 in year one. LLM features are cheap per user: at Claude Haiku 5.5 list prices of $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), 30 modest requests a month cost about 1.4 cents per user. The risk is free users, not payers, so any AI feature belongs behind the paywall or a tight quota. US external payment links currently carry zero Apple commission, but the rate is being re-litigated and Apple has proposed 5% for small developers ([MacDailyNews](https://macdailynews.com/2026/08/14/u-s-supreme-court-clears-path-for-app-store-commission-showdown-as-apple-must-defend-its-rates-in-lower-court/)); below $2,500 a month it is not worth building a web checkout around. A US individual can sell without an LLC, with the trade-off that the legal name appears as the seller, and Apple collects and remits US sales tax on App Store sales ([Afternoon](https://www.afternoon.co/blog/apple-app-store-sales-tax-guide)).

## Fitlog would fight Hevy's free tier and Fitbod's 290,000 ratings

Selling Fitlog as it stands is not a viable business. The strength-tracking market has two tiers and profitable incumbents hold both. The cheap logger tier is Hevy at $23.99 a year or $74.99 lifetime, with a free tier that already includes unlimited logging, and Strong at $29.99 a year or $99.99 lifetime; each has more than 100,000 US ratings ([Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350), [Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)). The workout-generation tier is Fitbod at $95.99 a year with 290,000 ratings and MacroFactor Workouts, launched in January 2026 at $71.99 a year ([Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543), [GainFrame](https://gainframe.app/blog/macrofactor-workouts-review/)). Fitbod claims $25M+ in annual recurring revenue with about 40 staff at cash-flow breakeven, and Hevy reached roughly $2M a year bootstrapped with nine people ([Fitbod](https://fitbod.notion.site/Built-for-Better-1fbef930ec4242e791426e0e2ca6ac34), [Ottawa Business Journal](https://obj.ca/fitness-app-entrepreneur-pumped-by-hevys-progress-to-2m-in-annual-revenue)). These companies cannot be outlasted, and their rating counts are a moat in search.

There is a real wedge, and it is narrow. Fitbod's recurring complaints are price increases without matching improvement, buggy Apple Watch sync, and lock-out of logging when the subscription lapses ([OnClarity](https://www.onclarity.com/leaderboard/insight/fitbod), [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/1041517543/reviews/12)). "Cheaper than Fitbod" is already served for logging, but cheap workout generation with a one-time price is less served, and that is what Fitlog does. Against that, published indie results for workout apps launched in 2024 to 2026 cluster between zero and about $100 in monthly recurring revenue: Just Log at about $20, FitnessLi at $57 from 8 subscribers, VigiGym with no paying users ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/justlog), [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/fitnessli), [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/vigigym)). The one outlier, Maxed AI, reports a run-rate of $13,000 to $14,000 a month at $24.99 a year behind a hard paywall, and its growth came from the founder's own short-form gym content plus paid ad tests ([Stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/blog/he-ignored-100-rivalsand-hit-13k-mrr)). That figure is second-hand and unaudited, and it describes a second content brand, which conflicts directly with Habesha AI as the owner's priority.

The gap between "works for me" and "sellable" is also larger than it looks. Incumbents ship Apple Watch apps with complications, and exercise media carries license strings: ExerciseDB's terms prohibit storing its content, MuscleWiki includes commercial use only on paid plans, and wger's licenses vary per exercise ([ExerciseDB](https://exercisedb.notion.site/ExerciseDB-API-Terms-of-Use-226983b728ca8090bf7be79564e4b356), [MuscleWiki](https://api.musclewiki.com/)). Whether Fitlog's current exercise media is cleanly licensed needs checking before any release.

The more promising asset inside Fitlog is the piece nobody else emphasizes: the body-fat goal tracker reading smart-scale data from Apple Health. Happy Scale, a weight-trend app built on Apple Health, charges $11.99 a year or $39.99 lifetime, says it supports three full-time people, and shows no body-fat or recomposition focus on its listing ([Happy Scale](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/happy-scale/id532430574)). That is an inference from a listing, not revenue data, and dedicated body-composition competitors were not surveyed. Other adjacent fitness niches look worse: GLP-1 trackers had a solo-founder success in Shotsy but are now crowded with clones and face Apple's rule restricting dosage calculators to approved entities, and Hyrox has a funded specialist with 170,000 users ([TAMradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/roxfit-seed-1-9m-hyrox)).

## The Habesha niche is real, small and filling fast

The addressable audience is modest. The US counts 372,804 people of Ethiopian ancestry, 278,182 of them born in Ethiopia, and about 71,777 Eritreans ([Wikipedia, citing the Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritreans)). Adding Canada, the UK, Europe and Australia brings the official total to roughly 0.8 to 1.0 million across all ages and religions. The Eritrean diaspora skews European, with 84,000 in Germany and 65,329 in Sweden. The category's ceiling on the App Store is visible in ratings: the largest Habesha-specific app, an Amharic Bible, has **9,544 US ratings after 14 years**, and most apps have under 500 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/holy-bible-in-amharic/id534343507)). No public revenue figure exists for any app in the niche, and Google Play, likely larger for this audience, was not examined.

The ratings tell a consistent story: utility and scripture beat learning by a wide margin, and first-generation adults who already read Amharic are the proven users.

**Language learning** is the trap. Duolingo has no Amharic course and a petition asks for one ([Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/add-amharic-language-to-duolingo)), which makes the gap look obvious, and that is exactly why it is closing. Selam launched in July 2026 with Amharic and Tigrinya, reached 144 US ratings at 4.8 stars in four months, and tests price points of $12.99 and $79.99 to $89.99 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/selam-learn-amharic-tigrinya/id6772249785)). LomiSpeak, Koko, Dubbadhu and Amharic Academy all arrived between June and September. The paying base is thin: Preply lists 110 Amharic tutors averaging about $9 to $11 an hour with 583 reviewing customers, so a $13 monthly subscription competes with a live lesson ([Preply](https://www.preply.com/en/online/amharic-tutors)). The technology is also uneven. Amharic speech recognition runs at 23% to 50% word error rate on independent tests and Tigrinya at 35% or worse, and no first-party commercial Tigrinya voice was found, so pronunciation scoring and AI-voiced Tigrinya are not reliable ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23654v1)). An adult learning app would be a late entry into a small market against well-reviewed, actively updated rivals.

**The Ethiopian calendar** is taken. The leader has 4,513 US ratings at 4.7 stars, shipped an update two days before this research, and the same developer owns the Eritrean calendar ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ethiopian-calendar-converter/id1466186227), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/eritrean-calendar-tigrinya/id1466526408)). A standalone converter also risks the 4.3(b) sameness rule.

**The Orthodox Tewahedo companion** is the clearest product gap. Current offerings are single-purpose: a prayer book with 1,462 ratings, a liturgy app with 387, the church-affiliated Mahibere Kidusan app with 168, a Senksar app with 41, and mezmur lyrics apps under 50 each ([Kidase Bet](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kidase-bet/id6478123184), [Tewahedo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tewahedo/id720274202), [Sinksar](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sinksar/id6504658468)). A US App Store search for "wudase mariam" returned no Tewahedo app at all. Nothing combines a fasting calendar, the daily Senksar, core prayers and mezmur lyrics with Ge'ez script, transliteration and English side by side, which is what a US-raised generation needs. The community exists and is growing: US Ethiopian Orthodox parishes rose from 101 to 197 between 2010 and 2020, with about 44,145 regular attendees ([US Religion Census](https://www.usreligioncensus.org/sites/default/files/2023-03/Appendix_F--Orthodox_Christian_Groups.pdf)), and an AP feature documents churches working to keep Ge'ez alive among US-raised youth ([AP](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/04/16/an-ethiopian-orthodox-church-in-washington-preserves-ancient-traditions-amid-modern-challenges/)). The limits are just as clear. The nearest analogue, Coptic Reader, is church-published and free with 10,686 US ratings, which signals that the dominant faith app in a small Orthodox diaspora tends to be institutional ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/coptic-reader/id649434138)). The only premium-priced Tewahedo app, at $17.99, has 43 ratings, and a RevenueCat-verified small Christian app earns $349 a month from 122 subscribers ([Mezgebe Haymanot](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mezgebe-haymanot/id1490688435), [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/growing-ios-christian-faith-app-recurring-revenue)). Diaspora parishes are also split three ways by jurisdiction, so content choices carry politics ([CNEWA](https://cnewa.org/magazine/americas-horn-of-africa-33368/)).

**Kids' Fidel** has the right buyer (a first-generation parent with income) and stale incumbents rated 3.3 to 4.3, but Lijoch's $19.99 unlock has drawn only 62 US ratings in six years and Temari, Gelada, Fidel Scribe and a Ge'ez alphabet app all launched in October and November 2026 ([Temari](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/temari-learn-amharic/id6829364535), [Gelada](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gelada-learn-amharic-fidel/id6824203790)). **Tigrinya and Eritrean** products are the emptiest shelf. Eritrean users pay small one-off prices for scarce tools (a $4.99 Tigrinya app has 350 ratings, a $6.99 dictionary keyboard 104), and Eritrean apps collect as many ratings in Canada, the UK, Germany and Sweden combined as in the US ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tigrinya-tigre-app/id944231639), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tigrinya-dict-keyboard/id1460741438)). The audience is smaller, needs European storefronts, and requires native Tigrinya content that AI cannot supply reliably. Keyboards, remittance, dating and recipes are respectively declining, licensed and funded, network-dependent, and without evidence of demand.

## Five ranked bets, none likely to reach $1,000 a month

The ranking below weighs three things: whether the owner has a distribution edge, how differentiated the app is under Apple's sameness rule, and how much new work it needs. The revenue ranges and odds are this report's judgment, anchored on the base rates above (median $72 a month, 17.3% reaching $1,000 among counted apps, typical indie fitness apps under $100) and on the niche's ratings ceiling. They are estimates, not measurements, and no Habesha app publishes revenue to check them against.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tewahedo daily companion, Amharic and English | $30 to $300 | $500 to $1,500 | 5% to 10% |
| 2 | Body-fat goal tracker carved from Fitlog | $0 to $150 | $400 to $2,000 | 3% to 5% |
| 3 | Eritrean Orthodox and Tigrinya edition | $20 to $150 | $300 to $600 | 1% to 3% |
| 4 | Fitlog as a one-time-price workout generator | $0 to $100 | $500 to $1,500 | 2% to 4% |
| 5 | Kids' Fidel tracing and reading app | $10 to $100 | $200 to $500 | 1% to 2% |

**1. Tewahedo daily companion.** One app with today's fast status and the fasting calendar, the daily Senksar reading, Wudase Mariam and core prayers, and mezmur lyrics, each shown in Ge'ez script with transliteration and English. Price it free at the core with a one-time supporter unlock around $9.99 to $19.99 and an optional annual tier, matching the niche's $1.99 to $29.99 norms; a hard paywall on prayers would damage trust. The arithmetic for the likely case: 5,000 to 10,000 installs over the first year would be a strong result against Bete Tselot's 1,462 US ratings in 15 years, and 2% to 3% paying about $15 is $1,500 to $4,500 in total. The good case depends on parishes adopting it and on English-first positioning reaching converts and the wider Orthodox audience, where a pan-Orthodox English Bible app gathered 13,225 ratings in three and a half years ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/orthodox-bible/id6446212792)). The agent can build the calendar engine, the reader, widgets, notifications and the store listing. The owner must do everything that makes it trustworthy: source the texts and clear rights for the specific Amharic and English editions used (not researched here), recruit a priest, deacon or Sunday school teacher to review content, decide how to handle the jurisdiction split, and personally get it announced in parishes. This ranking assumes the owner has, or can build, a parish relationship. Without one, the edge disappears and the body-fat tracker moves to first.

**2. Body-fat goal tracker.** Extract Fitlog's Apple Health weight and body-fat reading and its goal tracker into a focused recomposition app: trend smoothing, a goal date, lean mass versus fat mass, widgets. It reuses shipped code, so the build is the cheapest on this list, and Health & Fitness converts best of any category. Price near Happy Scale, about $12 to $20 a year with a $40 lifetime option. The weakness is distribution: the owner has no audience for it, Habesha AI's viewers are the wrong audience, and it depends on search for terms such as "body fat tracker" against competitors that were not surveyed. The owner must run an ASO keyword check, post in fitness communities as a real user, and consider a small Apple Ads keyword test. Avoid accuracy claims and iCloud storage of health values.

**3. Eritrean Orthodox and Tigrinya edition.** The same companion in Tigrinya, with German and Swedish interface text and European storefronts, sold as a paid unlock in the $4.99 to $6.99 range that Eritrean users already pay. It is the emptiest niche and the smallest audience. Build it only as a second language pack if idea 1 gets traction, and only with a native Tigrinya reviewer and Eritrean church contacts, which the owner must supply.

**4. Fitlog as a one-time-price Fitbod alternative.** Ship Fitlog publicly at roughly $30 to $40 lifetime, positioned on workout generation without a subscription. It needs a reliable Apple Watch app, cleanly licensed exercise media, onboarding, and a name and listing that never use Fitbod's brand. The typical result is under $100 a month. The good case exists only if the owner becomes a gym content creator in the Maxed AI mold, which competes with Habesha AI for the same limited hours.

**5. Kids' Fidel.** A tracing and reading app with recorded native audio, sold to parents as a one-time unlock. Incumbents are weak, but four competitors launched in the last six weeks, the best-known paid example has 62 ratings after six years, speech scoring for children is not technically reliable, and Kids Category rules add unresearched review work. The owner would need native voice recordings and Sunday school or weekend-school partners.

Not worth building: an adult Amharic learning app, an Ethiopian calendar converter, an Amharic keyboard, a GLP-1 tracker, or any generic AI wrapper.

### The first step is a three-week parish test, not a build

Test idea 1 before committing to it, because its entire value rests on an assumption no data source covers: that churches and community trust will distribute an app. In week one, the owner talks to two or three Seattle-area parishes and one Sunday school teacher, shows a mock-up, and asks two questions: would you announce this, and who can review the content. In the same week, Claude Code builds a thin slice, only the fasting calendar and a daily Senksar entry in Amharic and English, and ships it through the existing TestFlight setup with a one-screen waitlist page that includes a "founding supporter, $9.99" pledge button. In weeks two and three, distribute the TestFlight link by QR flyer and announcement at those parishes, and post two or three Habesha AI reels about the build to measure what the brand's audience actually does. In parallel, check keyword popularity for "tewahedo", "wudase mariam", "senksar" and "ethiopian fasting" in an ASO tool, and note which reviewer and which text editions are usable.

Set the pass line in advance. A reasonable threshold is 200 TestFlight installs or waitlist signups, at least 20 supporter pledges, at least one clergy or teacher reviewer committed, and at least two parishes willing to announce. Clearing it justifies a four to six week full build and a Featuring Nomination timed to a major fast or to Ethiopian New Year. Falling well short, for example under 50 signups after two parish announcements, is a cheap and decisive answer that the community channel does not work for this owner, and the fallback is idea 2, which costs little because the code already exists. The whole test costs time, not money.

## Conclusion

The question assumed the hard part was finding an app an AI agent could build. The research inverts it: the agent can build all five, and so can everyone else, which is why 3,000 apps a day now arrive in a store whose downloads are flat. What cannot be generated is a deacon's review, a parish announcement, a native Tigrinya voice, or permission to use a translation, and those are the inputs that separate the handful of earning apps in this niche from the hundreds with single-digit ratings. The owner's scarce contribution is therefore not product direction but roughly a dozen conversations, and the parish test is designed to find out in three weeks whether he can and wants to have them.

Two expectations should be set now. First, even the best case here is a modest side income that takes a year or more, so the project only makes sense if it also serves something else: goodwill for Habesha AI, a credible "built by AI for our community" story for the reels, or a useful tool the community lacks. Second, the window in the diaspora niche is narrowing by the month, as the eight learning apps launched since June show. If the parish test passes, speed matters more than polish, and if it fails, the honest conclusion is that the answer to the original question is "probably little money", and that Fitlog has already paid for itself by replacing a $95.99 subscription.
