## Analysis (James, 2026-09-20)

The 79% headline understates Jev. The disagreement has two causes and neither is Jev being noisy.

**1. Haiku is the drifted reference.** 70 of the 150 Haiku-pass stories are not AI stories by Jev's gate (Game Boy music player, UN map, snacks brand, rugs, toothpaste, iPhone cases). That is the rubric drift already logged in TASKS.md line 498. Jev fails 27 of the 45 stories that Haiku passed and Jev failed on exactly that ground. Measured against Haiku those count as disagreements. Measured against the brand they are corrections.

**2. The rest of the gap is the marginal band.** Split by Haiku's own average:

| Haiku average | n | agree |
|---|---|---|
| 5.5 to 7.0 (marginal) | 122 | 61% |
| outside that band (clear pass or clear fail) | 178 | 92% |

On clear cases Jev and Haiku agree. In the marginal band the AI-story disagreements are corporate and product announcements Haiku let through at 6.0 to 7.1 (a company invests in a data centre, GPT-6 rollout, a fintech launches an agent, a VC backs a security startup, a conference stage lineup). Jev scores them low on brand_fit and relevance. The brand rubric says corporate PR is off brand. Jev is reading the rubric as written.

Split by the AI gate: AI stories 81/110 agree (74%), non-AI stories 157/190 agree (83%).

**Systematic offsets to keep in mind** (Jev mean minus Haiku mean): story_structure +1.6, entertainment +1.1, brand_fit -1.2, relevance -1.1, grandmother -0.8. Average score correlation is 0.80 with mean absolute error 0.81. Jev is consistent with itself (their docs say run-to-run variance is near zero), so these offsets are stable and can be absorbed by thresholds on Jev's own distribution rather than by matching Haiku.

**Pass rate.** Jev with the AI gate passes 73 of 300 in this stratified sample. Weighted back to the real pool (22% Haiku-pass) that is roughly a 20% pass rate, the same order as today.

**Cost and speed, measured.** 155 ms median per story, 2,519 input tokens, $0.000106 per story. The full 300-story run cost three cents and took under a minute. Re-scoring the whole 12,614-story corpus is about $1.30.

**Gold set.** 9 of 10 agree. The miss is a genuine pass (AI delusion wrecks lives, Jev 6.09 vs 6.0 threshold) scored on a 200-char RSS stub, which the pipeline would never do in production.

**What the posted stories would say.** The 103 posted stories do not link back to a raw article with a cached body (they were hand-picked from Drive and Mike, not from the pipeline queue), so Jev could not be checked against Bisrat's real picks offline. That check needs bodies fetched for the posted URLs. Phase 1 item.

### Recommendation

Do not treat 85% agreement with Haiku as the bar. Haiku is not ground truth and half the gap is Haiku's drift. The bar for Phase 1 should be: on stories Bisrat actually picks, Jev's pass rate is at least as high as Haiku's, and Jev's AI gate removes the off-topic passes.

Proceed to Phase 1 shadow mode as planned: Jev scores every collected story alongside Haiku for two weeks, nothing user-facing changes, and each day's pending-research list shows both verdicts and the AI gate. Two rubric fixes to make first, both one-line:

1. Mike confirms the AI-story boundary paragraph. The gate already works on this sample; it needs his sign-off on edge cases (semiconductors, EVs, biotech with an AI component).
2. Mike reviews the five brand_fit levels. Jev reads "corporate PR" literally and fails funding rounds and product launches. If Bisrat wants some of those through (GPT-6 launch), the level text needs to say which.
