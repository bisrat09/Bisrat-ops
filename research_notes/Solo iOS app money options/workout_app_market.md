# Workout-tracker app market on iOS: can a solo dev sell a Fitbod competitor, and which adjacent niches are more winnable

Research date: 2026-11-13. US App Store prices unless stated. "Estimate" means a third-party model (AppGoblin, ScreensDesign, Adapty paywall library, AppSprint, ZoomInfo, Growjo); these disagree with each other by 10x or more and should be treated as order-of-magnitude at best. "Company-stated" means the company or founder said it; none of the figures here are audited.

Source-quality warning that applies throughout: most 2026 pricing/comparison pages that rank for these queries are written by competitors (SensAI, Dr. Muscle, Boostcamp, Lyfta, GainFrame). Their prices matched the App Store listings where I could cross-check, but their characterizations of user sentiment are marketing.

## 1. Competitors: pricing, revenue, downloads, rating counts, team size

### Takeaway
The category is split between a cheap logger tier (Hevy $23.99/yr, Strong $29.99/yr, both with lifetime options under $100) and an expensive workout-generation tier (Fitbod $95.99/yr, MacroFactor Workouts $71.99/yr, Ladder), and both tiers are held by profitable, entrenched companies. Fitbod itself claims $25M+ ARR with about 40 staff, and Hevy got to roughly $2M/yr bootstrapped with nine people; I found no confirmed revenue for any solo-run logger such as Strong or Liftin'.

### Cited Findings

Fitbod
- US App Store: 4.8 stars, 290K ratings, Apple Watch app (watchOS 10+), version 8.39.0 released Nov 5. IAP list shows current $15.99/month and $95.99/year alongside older $12.99/month and $79.99/year SKUs and several "Legacy Pricing" SKUs from $7.99 to $59.99 — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543)
- No free tier; 7-day trial requires a card; after trial, past workouts are viewable but new ones cannot be logged. Lifetime is "promo-only, no price published" (prices verified 2026-09-08 by a competitor blog) — [SensAI pricing comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison); a different guide lists a $359.99 lifetime option — [thepricer.org](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-fitbod-cost/). These conflict; the lifetime price is unconfirmed.
- Price went from $12.99/month and $79.99/year to $15.99/month and $95.99/year (reported as a 2025 increase by review sites; the legacy SKUs on the App Store listing corroborate that the older prices existed, but I found no primary announcement with a date) — [SensAI Fitbod review](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitbod-review-2026), [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543)
- Company-stated: scaled to "$25M+ ARR with only 40 employees while maintaining cash-flow breakeven" (undated company essay) — [Fitbod "Built for Better"](https://fitbod.notion.site/Built-for-Better-1fbef930ec4242e791426e0e2ca6ac34)
- Outside funding is small: $4.66M over 3 rounds, last round March 2020 — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/fitbod/financials); other aggregators say $5.06M to $5.3M — [Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/company/fitbod/funding)
- Third-party revenue estimates are far lower and inconsistent ($7.6M ZoomInfo, $7.9M Growjo) — [Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/Fitbod), [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/fitbod-inc/442596950). Dealroom claims 300,000+ paid subscribers, unverified — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/fitbod)

Hevy
- US App Store: 4.9 stars, 103K ratings; Pro $2.99/month (a $3.99 SKU also listed), $23.99/year, $74.99 lifetime; listing claims "15M+ lifters" and "112M+ workouts logged in 2025"; Apple Watch app with complications — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350)
- Free tier: unlimited logging, 4 routines, 7 custom exercises, 3 months of graph history, no ads — [SensAI pricing comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison)
- Company-stated (March 2023, dated): 1.5M users, expected 2023 revenue $2M (double 2021), nine staff, bootstrapped, owned 50/50 by co-founders Desmond McNamee and Guillem Ros; launched 2019 — [Ottawa Business Journal](https://obj.ca/fitness-app-entrepreneur-pumped-by-hevys-progress-to-2m-in-annual-revenue)
- Founder interview (June 2023, dated): 2M+ downloads in about four years, half in the prior five months; about $15k total ad spend; deliberately low prices give low LTV, offset by social/viral loops; bootstrapped and profitable — [RevenueCat Sub Club](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/guillem-ros-hevy-podcast)
- Early scale reference: a 2022 teardown cited the founders saying about $20K/month across iOS and Android — [ASO Expert "Boring Report"](https://aso-expert.kit.com/posts/boring-report-workout-tracking-app-makes-40k-a-month). A Starter Story profile gives $160K MRR (undated, source of figure unclear) — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/hevy-breakdown)
- No current (2025-2026) confirmed revenue found.

Strong
- US App Store: 4.9 stars, 109K ratings; seller Strong Fitness PTE Limited; Pro $4.99/month, $19.99/6 months, $29.99/year ($23.99 variant), "Forever" $99.99 ($79.99 variant) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)
- Free tier: unlimited logging, 3 custom routines, charts gated — [SensAI pricing comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison)
- Released September 2011; estimates of 10.5M installs and 832.5K MAU (older snapshot) — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/464254577)
- Revenue estimates range from "$10K+/month" (AppGoblin) to about $450K-$500K/month — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/discover/apps/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/). A competitor blog puts it near $7M/yr — [Jacked competitor analysis](https://jacked-blog.vercel.app/blog/jacked-competitor-analysis-2026). None are confirmed.

Jefit
- Elite $12.99/month, $69.99/year (first year shown at $52.49), no lifetime; free tier is ad-supported with 1,400+ exercises — [SensAI pricing comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison)

MacroFactor Workouts (major 2026 entrant)
- Launched January 12, 2026 on both stores — [MacroFactor Jan 2026 update](https://macrofactor.com/mm-jan-2026/), [MacroFactor Workouts](https://macrofactorapp.com/workouts/)
- Standalone $11.99/month, $47.99/6 months, $71.99/year, 7-day trial (third-party review, checked 2026-09-23) — [GainFrame review](https://gainframe.app/blog/macrofactor-workouts-review/)
- Bundle with the nutrition app $89.99/year for customers starting on or after 2026-01-01; earlier subscribers get Workouts free through 2027-01-12 — [MacroFactor help center](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/393-how-macrofactor-subscriptions-and-bundles-work), [MacroFactor price page](https://macrofactor.com/workouts/price/)

Ladder (venture-scale, not an indie comparable)
- Raised $105M (reported November 2024) — [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/ladder-raises-105m-for-strength-training-app/)
- Sensor Tower: US iOS weekly revenue spiked to $1.1M in late December 2025, with about 267K active users at quarter end — [Sensor Tower Q4 2025 US iOS top 5 H&F](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-ios-top-5-health-and-fitness-revenue-us-600af518241bc16eb8dce802)
- Estimate: about $6M/month App Store revenue and 300K monthly downloads (excludes web billing) — [AppSprint](https://appsprint.app/ladder-strength-training-plans-monthly-downloads)

Smaller loggers
- Liftin': developer listed as an individual, Valter Hemmi; released May 2019; freemium — [AppTail](https://apptail.io/app/liftin-gym-workout-tracker-xoT/canada). No revenue figure found.
- Setgraph: estimates conflict — about 30K monthly downloads and $30K/month ([Adapty paywall library](https://adapty.io/paywall-library/setgraph/)), $20K ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/setgraph-workout-training-log/)), or under $10K/month with 387.8K installs ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/1209781676))
- Gymverse: published by FITNESS22 LTD (a multi-app publisher, not an indie); estimates range from "$10K+/month" ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/1048454034)) to $700K with 70K installs ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/gymverse-gym-workout-planner/))
- Liftosaur: cited as offering a lifetime tier around $70 to $100 — [Dr. Muscle](https://dr-muscle.com/fitbod-cost/) (competitor blog; not verified against the App Store)
- Boostcamp: free tier described as permanent (Boostcamp's own page) — [Boostcamp](https://www.boostcamp.app/alternatives/fitbod)

### Inferences
- Price anchors a new entrant has to live between: $24-$30/yr and $75-$100 lifetime for a logger; $70-$96/yr for an app that generates workouts. Fitlog generates workouts, so it is functionally in the expensive tier, which is where undercutting has the most room.
- The incumbents are capital-efficient and profitable (Fitbod: about $5M raised against a claimed $25M+ ARR; Hevy: bootstrapped). They cannot be outlasted on burn, and Hevy's free tier already covers unlimited logging.
- Hevy's own founder says low price gave them low LTV and they survived on virality and a social graph. A solo app with no social loop and a low price inherits the low LTV without the distribution.
- Rating counts (290K, 109K, 103K) are a moat in App Store search; a new listing ranks behind these for "workout tracker" and "gym log" terms.

### Gaps
- Not researched or no usable data: Caliber, Alpha Progression, Motra, Boostcamp paid pricing, Ladder consumer pricing. Searches returned nothing reliable within the call budget.
- Strong's team size and any founder-stated revenue: not found. The "solo/tiny team" framing for Strong and Liftin' is unconfirmed beyond the Liftin' developer being listed as an individual.
- No Sensor Tower or Appfigures figure for Fitbod, Hevy or Strong was publicly visible (paywalled). Download counts for 2025-2026 are therefore missing for most apps.
- Fitbod's $25M+ ARR statement is undated.

## 2. User complaints about Fitbod and the others

### Takeaway
The recurring Fitbod complaints in sources I could reach are price rises without matching improvement, Apple Watch sync bugs, billing/cancellation friction, and workout recommendations that experienced lifters ignore. I could not retrieve any Reddit thread directly, so the Reddit evidence asked for is missing and the complaint picture rests on App Store reviews, review-aggregation sites and one forum thread.

### Cited Findings
- A review-analysis site summarizes Fitbod reviews as saying the subscription price "keeps increasing without any noticeable improvements" — [OnClarity Fitbod insight](https://www.onclarity.com/leaderboard/insight/fitbod)
- App Store reviews call Apple Watch to iPhone syncing "incredibly buggy" and describe logged sets resetting to 0 mid-workout — [AppsHunter Fitbod reviews](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/1041517543/reviews/12); Fitbod maintains a help article for watch sync problems — [Fitbod help center](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/4404213305623-I-m-having-issues-with-Fitbod-syncing-with-my-Apple-Watch-What-can-I-do)
- Billing: Trustpilot reviewers report being charged after cancelling because the subscription had to be cancelled through the original channel (web, App Store or Google Play) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/fitbod.me); complaint-site posts cite charges after cancellation, slow support, and repetitive workouts — [Sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/fitbod-us/fitbod-charged-me-after-cancellation-and-made-gettinga-refund-difficult), [Sikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/en/fitbod-us/fitbod-gave-me-repetitive-workouts-and-an-unreliable-app-experience)
- A MacPowerUsers commenter says Fitbod's AI "is not very intelligent" and that, per the Fitbod subreddit, many experienced users only log in it and ignore recommendations — [MacPowerUsers forum](https://talk.macpowerusers.com/t/gym-iphone-app-recommendation/32667)
- Visible App Store reviews (Dec 2024) complain that you cannot navigate the app during a workout, that personal records are hard to find, and that sharing forces Fitbod promotion — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543)
- TechRadar's review calls Fitbod more expensive than many competitors though cheaper than a trainer — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/fitbod-app-review)
- Strong: reviewers call the subscription "the most annoying part of the app" (2019), note the plate calculator only handles common bar weights (2020), and want a more flexible exercise database (2023) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)
- Hevy: visible critical reviews are minor feature requests inside 5-star reviews (bodyweight volume, date picker) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350)
- Jefit has no watch app on its free tier per a competitor comparison — [SensAI pricing comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison)

### Inferences
- Fitbod's weak points (price, watch reliability, lock-out of logging when the subscription lapses) are specific and addressable. Hevy and Strong show little visible dissatisfaction; competing with them on logging quality alone has no obvious wedge.
- Fitbod's 4.8 average over 290K ratings means complainers are a minority; the dissatisfied segment is real but its size is unknown.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit evidence. Searches for r/fitbod, r/Hevy, r/weightroom and r/iosapps threads returned no Reddit pages (the search tool did not surface reddit.com results). Claims about "what Reddit says" in this file are second-hand.
- No evidence gathered on data-export complaints for any app.
- No quantified share of 1-star reviews by theme.

## 3. Demand for a cheaper or one-time-purchase Fitbod alternative; indie launches 2024-2026 with revenue numbers

### Takeaway
Cheaper and one-time options already exist and are widely recommended (Hevy lifetime $74.99, Strong Forever $99.99, Liftosaur lifetime), so "cheaper than Fitbod" is a served need for logging; what is less served is cheap workout generation. Published indie revenue for 2024-2026 workout apps clusters at $0 to about $100 MRR, with one self-reported outlier at roughly $13K/month whose economics I could not verify.

### Cited Findings
- Review sites steer price-sensitive Fitbod users to Strong or Hevy, noting neither generates workouts ("that's the tradeoff") — [Dr. Muscle](https://dr-muscle.com/fitbod-cost/); AlternativeTo lists free alternatives first — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/fitbod/)
- Lifetime purchases exist at the incumbents: Hevy $74.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350); Strong $99.99/$79.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)
- Maxed AI (solo founder Max Hirsch, iOS strength logger, built with AI-assisted tools and no prior mobile experience): reported run-rate of $13,000-$14,000/month about six months after launch; $24.99/year; hard upfront paywall with no free tier or trial; growth from founder-led short-form gym content plus Meta/TikTok ad tests. The figure is relayed from a Starter Story feature and described as "not audited profit or guaranteed recurring revenue" — [Stork.ai, 2026-10-13](https://www.stork.ai/blog/he-ignored-100-rivalsand-hit-13k-mrr)
- Just Log (minimalist tracker): launched 2025-06-25, about $20 MRR a week after iOS launch — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/justlog)
- VigiGym (minimalist iPhone + Apple Watch tracker): launched around November 2025, no paying users at time of post — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/vigigym)
- ImprvHealth (solo AI fitness coach, $4.99/month): about $100 MRR — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/imprvhealth)
- FitnessLi (workouts + nutrition): $57 MRR, 8 active subscriptions, RevenueCat-verified — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/fitnessli)
- HealthBuddy: about $14 MRR, no active subscriptions as of 2025-12-31 — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/healthbuddy?metric=mrr)
- Personal Best: fitness app near $5K MRR built by a part-time freelancing founder — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/building-a-5k-mrr-app-while-freelancing-to-keep-the-lights-on-JhX6p14De0nagI0j1Xg1) (details not read in full)
- Non-fitness indie benchmark for what a strong solo outcome looks like: HabitKit reported about $28K MRR and roughly $602K total revenue for 2025 — [Sebastian Röhl, 2025 review](https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2025-the-year-that-changed-everything)

### Inferences
- The Maxed AI numbers need care: at $24.99/year, $13K of true MRR would need roughly 6,200 active annual subscribers (13,000 x 12 / 24.99, my arithmetic). With annual-only billing a "monthly run-rate" is more likely monthly new-sale cash, which depends on continuous content/ads and is not recurring in the usual sense.
- The one outlier won on distribution (founder content) and a hard paywall, not on features or price. The typical outcome for a quietly published logger, based on the verified small examples, is under $100 MRR.
- "One-time purchase" is not a differentiator against Hevy or Strong. It could be against Fitbod specifically, if the app generates workouts, since Fitbod has no confirmed standing lifetime price.

### Gaps
- No direct demand measurement (search volume for "fitbod alternative", Reddit thread counts) was obtained.
- Maxed AI launch date and the primary Starter Story source were not read; figures are second-hand.
- Survivorship bias: failed launches rarely publish numbers, so the real median is likely below the examples listed.

## 4. Table-stakes features and exercise-media licensing

### Takeaway
Incumbents ship Apple Watch apps with complications and large exercise libraries, so a paid entrant without a reliable watch app starts behind. Free exercise media exists but with licence strings attached: wger is open but per-exercise licences vary, ExerciseDB's hosted terms forbid storing content, and MuscleWiki's API includes commercial use only on paid plans; I found no published price for licensing a professional exercise-video library.

### Cited Findings
- Hevy's watch app logs sets, runs rest timers, shows heart rate, offers watch-face complications, and saves workouts when the watch reconnects — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350)
- Fitbod ships an Apple Watch app (watchOS 10+) — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543)
- Jefit's free tier includes 1,400+ exercises — [SensAI pricing comparison](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitness-app-pricing-free-tier-comparison)
- Strong users complain when the plate calculator does not support non-standard bars, showing that plate calculators are expected and scrutinized — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577)
- An indie-ideas guide states HealthKit integration is now expected for fitness trackers — [theswiftk.it](https://theswiftk.it.com/ios-app-ideas/fitness) (vendor blog, low authority)
- wger: open-source exercise database with public API; licensing varies per exercise so each entry's terms need checking — [wger on FSF directory](https://directory.fsf.org/wiki/Wger), [exercemus/exercises](https://github.com/exercemus/exercises)
- ExerciseDB (now AscendAPI): free V1 API with 1,500 exercises with GIFs — [exercisedb.dev](https://exercisedb.dev/); terms say all content is the provider's exclusive property, the licence is revocable, and storing the content is prohibited — [ExerciseDB API Terms of Use](https://exercisedb.notion.site/ExerciseDB-API-Terms-of-Use-226983b728ca8090bf7be79564e4b356)
- MuscleWiki API: free tier of 500 calls/month; paid plans include commercial use with no extra licensing fee; includes video demonstrations (vendor claim) — [MuscleWiki API](https://api.musclewiki.com/)

### Inferences
- ExerciseDB's no-storage clause conflicts with an offline-capable native app that bundles or caches media; it is the riskiest of the three for a shipped paid app.
- An app built for one user (Fitlog) probably lacks licensed exercise media, onboarding, and a hardened watch app; those are the main build costs between "works for me" and "sellable".

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence gathered on Live Activities, widgets, iCloud sync or progressive-overload logic as explicit user expectations; the claim that they are table stakes is unverified here.
- No price found for commercial exercise-video licences, or for MuscleWiki's paid plans.
- Whether Fitlog's current exercise media has a clean licence is unknown and should be checked before any release.

## 5. Winnable sub-niches for a solo developer

### Takeaway
GLP-1 companion tracking is the only adjacent niche where I found a solo-founder success story (Shotsy, started by one user-founder and grown through Reddit), but it is now crowded with clones and has since taken funding. For Hyrox, rucking, sauna/cold plunge, protein and body-composition trackers I found either a funded competitor or no revenue evidence at all, so none can be called proven on this research.

### Cited Findings

GLP-1 companion trackers
- Shotsy was built by Aja Beckett for her own dose and side-effect logging, spread through "spreadsheet threads and subreddits" before official launch, grew via users sharing screenshots without advertising, is freemium, and later took a small pre-seed round to respond to competition — [RevenueCat blog, 2025-12-03, via Plushcap summary](https://www.plushcap.com/content/revenuecat/blog/revenuecat-how-shotsy-became-the-go-to-app-for-glp-1-tracking)
- Shotsy estimates conflict sharply: about $350K/month and 45K monthly installs — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/discover/apps/shotsy-glp-1-tracker/); versus 1.8M installs, 153.7K MAU and "$10K+" monthly — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6499510249)
- Many clones with small estimated numbers: Pep (47.5K installs, under $10K/month) — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6504788281); Pokii (33.3K installs) — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6746722120); also Shotly, Shotwise, Shot Pal, Shot, MeAgain — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6755091838), [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6740042404), [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6739467364), [AppTail](https://apptail.io/app/meagain-glp-1-tracker-app-rXkv/united-states)
- App Store rule relevant here: drug dosage calculators must come from a manufacturer, hospital, university, insurer, pharmacy or other approved entity, or have FDA approval — [App Store Review Guidelines 1.4.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

Hybrid / Hyrox
- ROXFIT raised a £1.9M seed for Hyrox-specific tracking and reached 170K users in under a year organically; no revenue disclosed — [TAMradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/roxfit-seed-1-9m-hyrox)
- Hyrox the event business reported roughly $130M-$140M revenue for 2025 — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/life/wellness/how-hyrox-turned-everyday-gym-training-into-a-130-million-juggernaut/), [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/finance/financial-teardown-hyrox-turned-training-140-million-global-sport/)
- A creator-economy blog's "1,200 subscribers at $14.99" figure is illustrative math, not data — [Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/hyrox-training-apps-7-features-creators-should-ship)

Body weight / body composition trackers
- Happy Scale (weight-trend app with Apple Health import/export): Deluxe $1.99/month, $11.99/year, Premium lifetime $39.99 on one listing (older listings show $3.49/month and $59.99 lifetime); the developer says it is maintained by three full-time people — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/happy-scale/id532430574)
- Nothing in the results showed Happy Scale offering body-fat or body-composition features — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/happy-scale/id532430574)

Sauna / cold plunge
- Sauner (sauna tracker): about 12 MAU and one rating per estimate — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6755998688). Go Polar is another sauna/cold plunge tracker using Apple Watch — [Indie Hackers, BMcks Apps](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/bmcks-apps). No revenue evidence.

### Inferences
- Happy Scale sustaining three full-time staff at about $12/year suggests a simple Apple Health trend tracker can become a real small business over many years (released 2012), and its apparent lack of body-fat focus is a possible opening for a recomposition tracker built on smart-scale data, which Fitlog already reads. This is an inference from a listing, not from revenue data.
- The Shotsy pattern (founder is the user, community-led launch, freemium) transfers to niches where the builder has personal credibility. Fitlog's owner is his own user for body-fat goal tracking, less so for GLP-1.
- GLP-1 trackers carry extra review risk (1.4.2 dosage rule, medical-claim scrutiny) and TikTok has been removing medical-claim content per the user's own experience, which limits a content-led launch there.
- Hyrox has a funded specialist with 170K users; entering now means competing with a venture-backed team for a still-small audience.

### Gaps
- No data at all for: rucking apps, strength-for-over-50s apps, physical-therapy logs, protein-only trackers. Searches returned nothing relevant; absence of results is not evidence of absence of competitors.
- No confirmed revenue for any niche app listed, including Shotsy.
- Body-composition tracker competitors beyond Happy Scale were not surveyed (for example scale-vendor apps and dedicated body-fat apps), so "possible opening" is untested.

## 6. Health & fitness subscription economics (RevenueCat and similar)

### Takeaway
RevenueCat's 2026 report puts the Health & Fitness median at 2.9% download-to-paid, 37.7% trial-to-paid, a median annual price near $40, and about $36 realized per payer after a year; across all categories only 17.3% of apps reach $1K monthly revenue within two years and the median app earns about $72/month a year after launch. Revenue is therefore a download-volume problem first.

### Cited Findings
All from [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/) unless noted (figures as extracted from the visible page; the page was truncated partway through):
- Health & Fitness trial-to-paid: 37.7% median, top quartile above 51.4%
- Health & Fitness download-to-trial by day 30: 6.9% median; top performers above 23%
- Health & Fitness download-to-paid by day 35: 2.9% median, top quartile above 6.2%
- Health & Fitness median prices: weekly $4.99, monthly $9.99, yearly about $39.94
- Health & Fitness revenue per install: $0.48 median at day 14, $0.66 at day 60
- Health & Fitness realized LTV per payer: $24.23 median after 1 month (top quartile above $39.00), $35.64 median after 1 year
- All categories: 17.3% of apps reach $1K monthly revenue within two years; 4.6% reach $10K
- All categories: among apps that get there, median 58 days to $1K and 109 days to $10K monthly revenue
- All categories: median monthly revenue one year after launch is about $72
- All categories: day-35 download-to-paid is 10.7% median for hard paywalls versus 2.1% for freemium
- All categories: trials of 17-32 days convert at 42.5% versus 25.5% for trials of 4 days or less — [SaaStr summary of the 2026 report](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)
- Report scope: 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Prior-year comparison: RevenueCat 2025 had H&F median trial-to-paid at 39.9%, top 10% at 68.3%, with iOS converting about 2x Android — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/?p=124212)
- Different dataset: Adapty 2026 (16,000+ apps) has Health & Fitness leading trial-to-paid at 35.0% against a 25.6% global average, and reports 95% of subscription revenue going to the top 10% of apps — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)

### Inferences
- Rough unit math at category medians (my arithmetic): 1,000 downloads x 2.9% = 29 payers x $35.64 = about $1,030 gross in the first year, before Apple's commission. At $0.66 revenue per install by day 60, $1,000/month of new revenue needs on the order of 1,500 installs a month.
- Hevy and Strong price 25-40% below the $39.94 category-median annual price, so a logger priced to undercut them would earn well under the median LTV per payer.
- The hard-paywall result (10.7% vs 2.1%) and the Maxed AI case point the same way: for a small app without a network effect, a paywall up front monetizes scarce traffic better than a generous free tier, at the cost of ratings volume and word of mouth.

### Gaps
- Health & Fitness 12-month retention by plan, refund rates, and category-specific time-to-$1K were not in the visible portion of the report.
- Median figures include apps with marketing budgets; a zero-marketing solo app likely sits below median on downloads, which the benchmarks do not show.

## 7. App Store rules that matter for health apps

### Takeaway
A workout logger that reads weight and body fat from HealthKit is on safe ground if it uses the data only for the user's benefit and makes no measurement or medical claims, but two clauses bite: 5.1.3(ii) says apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud", which constrains iCloud sync of HealthKit-derived data, and 1.4.2 restricts drug dosage calculators to approved entities, which matters for any GLP-1 pivot.

### Cited Findings
All quoted from [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched 2026-11-13; the final ~14K characters of the page were not read):
- 1.4.1: medical apps "that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny"; apps "must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements"; apps "should remind users to check with a doctor"
- 1.4.2: "Drug dosage calculators must come from the drug manufacturer, a hospital, university, health insurance company, pharmacy or other approved entity, or receive approval by the FDA or one of its international counterparts"
- 5.1.1(ix): apps in highly regulated fields including healthcare, "or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer"
- 5.1.2(vi): HealthKit data "may not be used for marketing, advertising or use-based data mining, including by third parties"
- 5.1.3(i): health and fitness data may not be used or disclosed to third parties for advertising or data mining; "You must disclose the specific health data that you are collecting from the device"
- 5.1.3(ii): "Apps must not write false or inaccurate data into HealthKit or any other medical research or health management apps, and may not store personal health information in iCloud"
- 2.5.1: "HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app", and apps should indicate that integration in their description
- 3.1.2(a): auto-renewable subscriptions "must provide ongoing value to the customer"; 3.1.2(c): clearly describe what the user gets before asking them to subscribe
- 1.1.6: false information and features, including inaccurate device data, are not allowed

### Inferences
- Fitlog's body-fat goal tracker should present smart-scale body fat as data read from Health, not as its own measurement, and avoid accuracy or medical-outcome claims to stay clear of 1.4.1.
- If Fitlog syncs via iCloud/CloudKit, HealthKit-sourced weight and body-fat values should be re-read from HealthKit on each device rather than stored in iCloud; whether a user's own workout log counts as "personal health information" under 5.1.3(ii) is not defined in the text I read and is a judgment call at review.
- 5.1.1(ix) is aimed at healthcare services; a fitness logger published by an individual is common practice (Liftin' is listed under an individual's name), but a GLP-1 or physical-therapy app sits closer to that line.
- Ad-funded or analytics-heavy monetization of HealthKit data is closed off by 5.1.2(vi) and 5.1.3(i); the business model has to be paid or subscription.

### Gaps
- Apple's HealthKit-specific developer documentation and App Privacy "nutrition label" requirements were not fetched.
- No evidence gathered on how strictly 5.1.3(ii) is enforced against CloudKit-synced fitness apps in practice.
- Non-Apple rules (FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, state consumer-health-data laws such as Washington's My Health My Data Act) were out of scope and not researched; they may apply to a Seattle-based developer handling body-composition data.
