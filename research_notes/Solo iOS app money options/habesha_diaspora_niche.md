# Market for iOS apps serving the Ethiopian and Eritrean (Habesha) diaspora, as of 2026-11-13

Method note: App Store figures below were pulled on 2026-11-13 from Apple's public iTunes Search/Lookup API (US storefront unless stated, plus CA/GB/DE/SE/AU lookups for key apps). Ratings counts are per storefront. "upd" = current version release date. In-app purchase (IAP) price points were scraped from the public US App Store page and are unlabelled price points, not confirmed tier names; treat as approximate. Each app is linked to its App Store page.

## 1. Audience size (population, income, iPhone share, heritage speakers, church communities)

### Takeaway
The addressable high-income-country diaspora is small: roughly 370K people of Ethiopian ancestry and about 70K Eritrean in the US by Census counts, plus low-to-mid tens of thousands each in Canada, UK, Germany, Sweden, Norway, Switzerland and Australia. A realistic official-count total for US + Canada + UK + EU + Australia is on the order of 0.8 to 1.0 million people (both origins, all ages, all religions), and no source quantifies how many second-generation members cannot read Fidel.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- Ethiopian ancestry: 372,804 (2023 ACS 1-year); Ethiopia-born: 278,182 (2023 ACS). In 2007-2011 the Ethiopia-born count was about 151,515, so the foreign-born population has nearly doubled in about 12 years — [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Americans, citing US Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans)
- A separate summary of 2019-2023 ACS data gives 283,075 Ethiopian immigrants, 0.6% of all US immigrants, with a 93% employment rate — [GMU Institute for Immigration Research](https://iir.gmu.edu/immigrant-stories-overview/ethiopia)
- Including second generation, MPI's Aaron Terrazas estimated "upwards of 460,000" (dated: 2007). MPI's 2014 RAD diaspora profile estimated about 255,000 first plus second generation and found household income "substantially lower" than the US average — [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans); [MPI Select Diaspora Populations](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/research/select-diaspora-populations-united-states)
- Community (unofficial) estimates run far above Census counts: DC area 75,000 "conservative" up to 250,000; Seattle 25,000-40,000 (Crosscut, 2017); Dallas-Fort Worth about 44,600-50,000; Columbus about 40,000; Clark County NV about 40,000; Minnesota 34,927 official — [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans)
- DC metro: about 53,500 Ethiopian immigrants (about 4% of the metro's immigrants); median household income $48,000 for Ethiopian-immigrant-headed households (fact sheet data year not clearly stated) — [iDod fact sheet, DC metro](https://d101vc9winf8ln.cloudfront.net/documents/39992/original/Ethiopian_Immigrants_in_the_DC_metro_iDod_Fact_Sheet.pdf)
- Eritrean, US: 71,777 (ACS 1-year 2024, table B02023, basis not specified on the page, likely ancestry/origin); older Eritrea-born counts were 39,063 (2015) and 25,848 (2007-2011) — [Wikipedia: Eritreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritreans); [Wikipedia: Eritrean Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Americans)
- Household income, conflicting secondary sources: CensusEasy gives about $75,170 national median household income for Ethiopian Americans (labelled 2024 ACS 1-year); ZipAtlas gives $89,640 (Ethiopian ancestry) and $88,687 (Ethiopia-born), vintage unclear — [CensusEasy](https://censuseasy.com/origins/ethiopian-americans); [ZipAtlas](https://zipatlas.com/us/income/ethiopian-median-household-income.htm). These conflict with each other and with the DC $48,000 figure; none was verified against the raw ACS table.
- Language at home: ACS publishes only a combined "Amharic, Somali, or other Afro-Asiatic languages" category nationally (table B16001_111/C16001), so Amharic and Tigrinya are not broken out in the standard tables — [US Census API, B16001 variable list](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/acs/acs1/groups/B16001.json); [Census language use](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/language-use/about.html)
- Older breakout: 43,125 Amharic speakers in the DC metro (2009-2013 ACS; dated) — [Census Bureau release CB15-185](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/archives/2015-pr/cb15-185.html)
- Seattle: within the city's Afro-Asiatic language group, Somali 37.7%, Amharic 25.0%, Oromo 21.5%, Tigrinya 16.3% — [City of Seattle OIRA, 2026 Commonly Spoken Languages](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/Departments/OIRA/2026%20Commonly%20Spoken%20Languages%20in%20Seattle-FINAL.pdf)
- Oromo: over 3,000 Oromo-speaking K-12 students in Minnesota; MPR estimate of about 40,000 ethnic Oromo in the state — [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/top-languages-spoken-at-home-in-minnesota-spanish-hmong-somali/601368610); [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans)

**Canada**
- 2021 census ethnic origin: Ethiopian 13,515, Amhara 2,165, Oromo 2,000, Tigrinya 2,570 (total 20,250); geography of this line is ambiguous in the article (may be Toronto CMA rather than national) — [Wikipedia: Ethiopian Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Canadians)

**Orthodox Tewahedo church abroad**
- US, Ethiopian Orthodox: parishes grew from 101 (2010) to 197 (2020); about 44,145 regular attendees, which the census says is 46% of adherents; 49% of parishes were founded since 2010 — [2020 US Religion Census, Orthodox appendix](https://www.usreligioncensus.org/sites/default/files/2023-03/Appendix_F--Orthodox_Christian_Groups.pdf); [Orthodox Reality 2020 census report](https://orthodoxreality.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/2020CensusGeneralReport1.pdf)
- Diaspora parishes are split three ways: in communion with Addis Ababa, with the North American synod, or independent — [CNEWA](https://cnewa.org/magazine/americas-horn-of-africa-33368/)
- Heritage-language pressure is visible in churches: a 2025 AP feature describes a DC-area Ethiopian Orthodox church working to preserve Ge'ez and tradition among US-raised youth — [AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/04/16/an-ethiopian-orthodox-church-in-washington-preserves-ancient-traditions-amid-modern-challenges/)

### Inferences
- US EOTC adherents implied by the Religion Census are roughly 96,000 (44,145 / 0.46). This is a back-calculation, not a reported number, and it is far below the roughly 370K Ethiopian-ancestry count, so registered/attending Orthodox members are perhaps a quarter of the US Ethiopian population (the rest are unaffiliated-in-census Orthodox, Protestant, Muslim, or non-practising).
- Summing the official counts found (US about 445K both origins; Canada about 55-60K; UK at least 25K Eritrea-born plus an unknown Ethiopia-born number; Germany/Sweden/Switzerland/Norway/Netherlands/Italy/Denmark about 260K Eritrean plus Ethiopian not found; Australia about 20-25K) gives roughly 0.8-1.0M in high-income Western countries. Community estimates would double that, but they are unsourced.
- If the US second generation is roughly ancestry minus foreign-born (372,804 - 278,182), that is about 95K US-born Ethiopian-ancestry people, the core "cannot read Fidel" segment for a learning app. Applying a general-population iPhone share of about 57% gives very roughly 50-55K US-born Ethiopian-ancestry iPhone users of all ages. This is a rough ceiling, not a measured figure.
- Eritrean diaspora skews more European (Germany, Sweden, Switzerland, Norway) than the Ethiopian diaspora, which is US-heavy. App Store ratings distributions confirm it: the Eritrean Calendar app has more ratings in DE (132) and SE (124) than in CA or GB, and about half its US count (239).

### Gaps
- No source found for the number or share of second-generation Ethiopians/Eritreans who cannot read Amharic or Tigrinya. Academic work on this group focuses on identity, not literacy; one author notes few studies exist on second-generation Ethiopian and Eritrean youth at all — [Springer, Toronto study](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s12134-015-0462-9)
- Raw ACS values could not be pulled directly (Census API now requires a key); US numbers above are via secondary sources citing ACS.
- No national count of Amharic, Tigrinya or Oromo home speakers in current ACS standard tables.
- Not found: Ethiopia-born in England and Wales 2021, Ethiopian populations in Germany/Sweden/Italy/Israel/Gulf, Eritrean Orthodox parish counts in the US or Europe, EOTC parish counts in Canada/Europe/Australia.
- No iPhone-share data specific to Ethiopian/Eritrean diaspora or African immigrants.
- Median household income is unresolved ($48K DC immigrant-headed vs $75K-$90K national from aggregator sites).

## 2. Existing apps by niche: quality, ratings count, last update, price and business model; big-brand coverage

### Takeaway
Every niche already has at least one app, but almost all are tiny: the entire category's ceiling on the US App Store is about 8,000-9,500 ratings (an Amharic Bible and an abandoned Amharic keyboard), and most apps have under 500. The most important 2026 change is a wave of new, actively updated learning apps (Selam, LomiSpeak, Koko, Dubbadhu, Amharic Academy, Temari, Gelada, Fidel Scribe) launched between June and November 2026, so "no modern Amharic/Tigrinya learning app" is no longer true.

## 4. AI quality for Amharic and Tigrinya (LLM text, TTS, ASR) as of 2026

### Takeaway
Amharic is workable but second-tier: commercial TTS exists (Azure neural voices, Google Gemini-TTS in preview) and ASR error rates are roughly 23-50% WER on independent tests; Tigrinya has no first-party commercial TTS found and ASR is worse (about 35-46% WER for the best open models). A fully AI-voiced tutor or kids' reading app with pronunciation scoring is risky; pre-recorded native audio plus LLM text is the safer architecture.

### Cited Findings
**ASR (speech recognition)**
- Ethio-ASR paper (March 2026), WAXAL test set, WER: Whisper-large-v3 153% Amharic / 166% Tigrinya (effectively unusable); SeamlessM4T-v2-large 104% / 100%; Meta MMS-1b-all 57.5% / 70.5%; Meta OmniASR-LLM-7B-v2 25.1% / 40.7%; best Ethio-ASR (w2v-BERT-2.0) 22.9% / 35.2%. The authors call Ethiopian languages "severely underrepresented in speech technology" and caution that OmniASR may have seen test data. Training data was only about 190 hours Amharic and 182 hours Tigrinya — [arXiv 2603.23654](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23654v1)
- Ethio-ASR models and code are openly released on Hugging Face — [Hugging Face: Ethio-ASR-mms-1b-uf](https://huggingface.co/boazsew/Ethio-ASR-mms-1b-uf)
- ElevenLabs Scribe v2 docs place Amharic in the "Moderate" accuracy band (over 25% to 50% WER); Tigrinya and Oromo are not listed. ElevenLabs' Amharic marketing page quotes much better WER (3.1% FLEURS in one locale, 29.9% for Scribe v1 in another); the marketing figures are inconsistent across locale versions and conflict with the docs band — [ElevenLabs STT docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/speech-to-text); contradicted by [ElevenLabs Amharic STT page](https://elevenlabs.io/speech-to-text/amharic)
- A January 2026 IEEE Access paper reports a phoneme-aware Transformer for Amharic with large WER reduction over prior Amharic Transformer systems (research, not a product) — [DOAJ listing](https://doaj.org/article/d2f773ff6a474918a0dfe606092b6818)

**TTS (text to speech)**
- Azure Speech has two Amharic neural voices: am-ET-MekdesNeural (female) and am-ET-AmehaNeural (male) — [Microsoft Learn language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/AZURE/ai-services/speech-service/language-support); [json2video voice listing](https://json2video.com/ai-voices/azure/voices/am-et-mekdesneural/)
- No Azure Tigrinya voice found; one third-party TTS app maps "Tigrinya" to the Amharic Azure voices as a fallback — [Hugging Face selam-tts README](https://huggingface.co/spaces/snackshell/selam-tts/blob/main/README.md?code=true)
- Google Cloud Gemini-TTS lists Amharic (am-ET) as Preview; Amharic was not seen in the Chirp 3 HD language snippets — [Google Cloud Gemini-TTS docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts?authuser=9); [Chirp 3 HD docs](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/chirp3-hd?hl=zh-TW)
- ElevenLabs Multilingual v2 (29 languages) and Flash v2.5 (32 languages) do not include Amharic, Tigrinya, Oromo or Somali. Eleven v3 supports 70+ languages but the fetched page did not list them — [ElevenLabs TTS docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech)
- Meta MMS-TTS open models exist for both Amharic and Tigrinya (community demo romanises Ge'ez script for better pronunciation; demo quality, not production) — [Hugging Face Space: MMS TTS African languages](https://minte-atnafu-gihontech-local-language-tts.hf.space)

**LLM text quality**
- IrokoBench (16 African languages including Amharic): about a 45% average performance gap between English and African languages across all LLMs tested; on AfriMMLU in-language, GPT-4o averaged 52.3 and Claude Opus 43.0 across African languages (2024 models; dated) — [arXiv 2406.03368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03368); [Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/afrimmlu)
- AfroBench (64 languages, 15 tasks) finds large gaps between English and African languages, tracking how much monolingual data each language has — [arXiv 2507.17974](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.17974v3)
- A November 2025 WiNLP paper on Afaan Oromo, Amharic and Tigrigna hate-speech detection found LLMs strong in English and weaker in these languages across five model families — [ACL Anthology 2025.winlp-main.31](https://aclanthology.org/2025.winlp-main.31)
- ProverbEval tests cultural/proverb understanding in Amharic, Tigrinya, Afaan Oromo and Ge'ez and shows results are sensitive to prompt language and answer ordering — [arXiv 2411.05049](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.05049)
- Uhura benchmark (includes Amharic): all models, including Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o and o1-preview, score better in English than in the African languages — [arXiv 2412.00948](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00948)

### Inferences
- Feasible today: Amharic text explanations, vocabulary, transliteration and translation via a frontier LLM (with human review for correctness), and Amharic TTS via Azure or Gemini-TTS for sentence playback.
- Not reliably feasible: scoring a child's or learner's spoken Amharic (WER of 23-50% on adult read speech means children's and heritage-accent speech will be worse), and anything voice-based in Tigrinya without recording native speakers.
- Whisper-based products (including OpenAI's API if it inherits Whisper behaviour) should not be assumed to work for Amharic or Tigrinya given the over-100% WER above; test before building.
- The benchmark papers found are mostly on 2024-era models. Frontier models from 2025-2026 are likely better at Amharic text, but no independent 2026 per-model Amharic/Tigrinya scores were found, so this is unverified.
- Because content quality cannot be delegated fully to AI, a founder who reads Amharic/Tigrinya natively (or has church/teacher reviewers) has a real edge over the generic template developers currently occupying the niche.

### Gaps
- No Amharic- or Tigrinya-specific scores for 2025-2026 frontier LLMs (GPT-5 class, Claude 4+ class, Gemini 2.5/3) were found.
- Could not confirm whether Eleven v3 includes Amharic; OpenAI TTS/transcribe language support for Amharic was not retrieved; Lesan AI's current product/API status was not found.
- No evaluation of Amharic TTS naturalness (MOS) for Azure or Gemini voices was found.
- No data on ASR accuracy for children's speech or heritage-speaker accents in either language.

## 6. Willingness to pay (tutors, weekend schools, church giving, app purchases)

### Inferences
- The tutor market is small in absolute terms: 583 reviewing customers on the largest platform's Amharic category suggests low thousands of paying online Amharic learners worldwide at any time, a mix of heritage learners, spouses/partners, adoptive parents and aid/diplomatic workers.

- No subscriber counts, conversion rates or churn for any Habesha app.
- No Preply listing data for Tigrinya; italki Amharic tutor count not retrieved.
