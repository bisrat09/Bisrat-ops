# Distribution Without Paid Ads and App Store Review Risk for AI-Built iOS Apps (US, as of 2026-11-13)

Reader context: solo owner in Seattle, paid Apple Developer account, native SwiftUI app on TestFlight built with Claude Code, small IG/TikTok/YouTube brand (hundreds of views per reel, Ethiopian/Eritrean diaspora audience), small budget.

Source-quality note for the writer: Apple pages, AppTweak, SplitMetrics, Appfigures, RevenueCat and TechCrunch/9to5Mac are treated as documented. Many pages that rank for "vibe-coded app rejection" and "TikTok organic for apps" are vendor marketing blogs with unsourced numbers; where I use them they are labelled "vendor blog, unverified". Items labelled "search-snippet only" were seen in search results and not opened in full.

## 1. App Store Optimization in 2025 to 2026: share of downloads from search, what matters, realistic long-tail numbers

### Takeaway
Search is still the main discovery surface on the App Store (Apple's own figure is "nearly 65%" of downloads directly after a search; older Sensor Tower data put non-game apps near 70%), but no source gives an indie-specific share and no reliable source gives downloads-per-day for long-tail keywords; the credible signals say long-tail keywords yield "a handful" of downloads a day at best. The 2025 to 2026 changes (AI-generated tags, screenshot text feeding discovery, keyword-linked custom product pages, Personalized Collections, Creative Assets, more ad slots in search) shift weight toward clear metadata and creatives, not away from keywords.

### Cited Findings
**Share of downloads from search**
- Apple said in its December 2025 Apple Ads announcement that "nearly 65 percent" of App Store downloads happen directly after a search; the same announcement said more ad placements would be added further down search results from early 2026 (March 2026 per later coverage). Confirmed via press coverage, not Apple's own page — [MacRumors, 2025-12-18](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/18/app-store-search-results-more-ads-2026/); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/apple-expands-app-store-search-ads-reach-high-intent-users)
- Older (2020 data): Sensor Tower found search accounted for 70% of installs for non-game apps in 2020 (56% for games in an earlier 2017-2018 study). This is the closest proxy for small utility-type apps, and it is six years old — [Sensor Tower download sources report 2021](https://sensortower.com/blog/app-store-download-sources-report-2021)
- Coverage also attributes to Apple a roughly 60% conversion figure for ads in the top search position (search-snippet only) — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/apple-widens-app-store-search-ads-capture-high-intent-demand)

**Supply and demand backdrop (matters for ASO competitiveness)**
- Appfigures estimates 557K new apps were released on the App Store in 2025, up about 24% from 448K in 2024, the biggest release year since 2016; it names AI coding tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Replit) as one of three drivers without quantifying it — [Appfigures, 2025-12-05](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/20251205?f=2)
- Appfigures data cited by TechCrunch: Q1 2026 iOS app releases up 80% year over year; April 2026 to date up 89% on iOS. Utilities moved to the No. 2 category for new releases, Lifestyle to No. 3, Productivity entered the top five, Health and Fitness is fifth — [TechCrunch, 2026-04-18](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/)
- Secondary coverage reports almost 560,000 new apps were added to the App Store in January to June 2026, while App Store downloads rose only about 3% last year and 2% so far in 2026 (search-snippet only) — [Gigazine, 2026-07-21](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260721-app-store-new-apps); [9to5Mac / TechSpot coverage](https://techspot.com/news/113213-apple-app-store-inundated-low-quality-vibecoded-apps.html)

**2025 changes (WWDC 2025 / iOS 26)**
- App Store Tags: generated by LLMs from app metadata and other sources, human-reviewed before going live; shown alongside categories on search pages and in search results. TechCrunch reported them live in the iOS 26 developer beta on 2025-06-14 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/14/the-app-stores-new-ai-generated-tags-are-live-in-the-beta); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/apple-unveils-major-app-store-connect-updates-at-wwdc-2025/)
- Apple said at WWDC25 that screenshots and other metadata feed discoverability and that the extraction uses AI rather than OCR; it advised against stuffing keywords into visual assets — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/14/the-app-stores-new-ai-generated-tags-are-live-in-the-beta); [AppTweak WWDC 2025 recap](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-wwdc-2025-recap?currency=us)
- Custom product pages can be assigned keywords from the current App Store version; when the app appears in organic search for those keywords, the custom page shows instead of the default — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/apple-unveils-major-app-store-connect-updates-at-wwdc-2025/); [Adapty WWDC25 summary](https://adapty.io/blog/wwdc25-what-apple-announced/)
- ASO blogs claimed keyword rankings shifted in June 2025 because of tags; this is inference from observed movement, not confirmed by Apple — [FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/apple-may-have-changed-its-recommendation-ruleswas-all-your-keyword-optimization-wasted/)

**2026 changes (WWDC 2026)**
- Personalized Collections: recommended-app groups across the Apps, Games and Search tabs based on interests, usage and downloads, each with an "App Note" explaining the suggestion; rollout began the week of 2026-06-15, English, US first. Apple has published no ranking framework — [AppTweak WWDC 2026 recap](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-wwdc-2026-recap)
- Creative Assets: rich images and video that can appear in the product page header and in organic keyword search results, separate from screenshots; managed through an Asset Library in App Store Connect, submitted and approved separately from app versions, reusable across custom product pages, In-App Events and Apple Ads — [AppTweak WWDC 2026 recap](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-wwdc-2026-recap)
- App Intents is now the only way to connect an app to the new Siri; SiriKit received a formal deprecation notice — [AppTweak WWDC 2026 recap](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-wwdc-2026-recap)
- A revised guideline edition dated 2026-10-16 reportedly added rule 2.3.14 governing those creative assets, including a ban on discount prices in them (search-snippet only; not verified against Apple's page) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/apples-rule-2-3-14-bans-discount-prices-in-app-store-creative-assets/)

**Long-tail keyword reality**
- Apple's keyword popularity is a relative index, not a search count. Appfigures uses a 5 to 100 scale where 5 means "little to no searches at all", says many keywords you look at will score 5, and advises avoiding single-digit popularity keywords — [Appfigures metrics glossary](https://help.appfigures.com/en/article/understanding-app-store-optimization-metrics-1pcnt7t/); [Appfigures keyword guide](https://appfigures.com/resources/guides/which-keywords-to-optimize-for)
- Unverified ASO-tool blog estimates (attribution between the two blogs is not certain from my search): popularity 16 to 20 keywords "barely register", and even rank 1 brings "a handful" of downloads daily; popularity 21 to 34 is estimated at about 1,000 to 2,000 searches a day; top 3 on a popularity-45 keyword is estimated at 50 to 200 downloads a day; a #1 position is assumed to capture 25 to 30% of search traffic — [Sonar blog](https://trysonar.app/blog/real-search-volume); [Appalize blog](https://www.appalize.com/blog/aso-strategies/app-store-keyword-search-volume-how-to-measure-use-it)
- One indie datapoint (Indie Hackers, May of an unstated recent year; page returned 403 so numbers are from the search snippet): 182 App Store impressions, 70 product page views, 11 downloads in the first 10 days (about 9% conversion as the developer computed it), with TikTok videos averaging about 250 views each — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/day-10-11-downloads-2-000-tiktok-views-and-the-mistake-that-killed-my-us-conversions-1dcfbbeff5)

### Inferences
- With roughly 65 to 70% of downloads following a search and new-app supply up 80%+ year over year against flat downloads, average organic downloads per new app are falling; a new app with no ratings should expect single-digit to low-double-digit daily downloads from long-tail search at best, and often near zero in the first weeks.
- For a diaspora-language or culture-specific app, the realistic ASO win is low-competition, low-volume terms (Amharic, Tigrinya, Ethiopian, Eritrean, Habesha plus function words). These are likely popularity 5 to 20 keywords: easy to rank, small in volume.
- Because Apple now reads screenshot text with AI and assigns tags from metadata, plain-language captions in screenshots and a precise subtitle are probably more valuable than they were before 2025; this is consistent with Apple's statements but Apple has published no weighting.
- Keyword-linked custom product pages are a free tool that fits a niche app (for example, a separate page for an Amharic-speaker audience versus a general audience).

### Gaps
- No source gives the share of indie-app downloads from search specifically; all figures are store-wide or non-game.
- No reliable, primary-source table of downloads per day by keyword popularity and rank. Appfigures and AppTweak sell these estimates inside their tools.
- I found no documented numbers on how ratings count affects ranking for small apps, or on title versus subtitle versus keyword-field weights in 2026; these remain practitioner lore.
- Tag behaviour since launch (whether developers can edit or remove tags, measured traffic from tags) was not verified.

## 2. Organic short video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) as an app growth channel

### Takeaway
I found no trustworthy, independent dataset on view-to-install conversion for organic short video; nearly everything that ranks is vendor marketing. The few honest indie datapoints show that accounts at a few hundred views per video produce close to zero installs, and platform vendors themselves concede that even large view counts convert poorly when the audience is wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Indie Hackers build-in-public log: TikTok videos averaging about 250 views each, about 2,000 views total, 11 App Store downloads in 10 days from all sources; the developer concluded organic reach alone was not converting (search-snippet only; page 403) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/day-10-11-downloads-2-000-tiktok-views-and-the-mistake-that-killed-my-us-conversions-1dcfbbeff5)
- A TikTok-distribution vendor states the failure mode directly: "A video can hit 500K views and generate 40 installs if it reaches the wrong audience", and argues the real constraint is measurement and attribution, recommending App Store Connect referral sources and per-account links. Vendor pitch for multi-account distribution; illustrative, not measured — [TokPortal](https://www.tokportal.com/verticals/tiktok-organic-distribution-mobile-apps-games)
- Adapty (2026): organic TikTok "can work well" if the app is visual and easy to understand; paid and organic TikTok are separate strategies, and paid TikTok is still uncommon as a main channel — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/meta-ads-subscription-apps/)
- Faceless formats named in vendor guides: screen-recorded tutorials, text-overlay storytelling, AI avatar presenters, product-only demos, stock footage plus voiceover; the same guide says faceless accounts typically grow more slowly than face-forward accounts in the first 90 days (vendor blog, unverified) — [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/faceless-tiktok-strategy-for-founders)
- TikTok paid minimums if boosting a winning organic post: campaign daily budget above $50 and ad group daily budget above $20 (reported from TikTok's help center via a newsletter) — [Growth Gems #147](https://growthgems.substack.com/p/growth-gems-147-tiktok-ads-and-caveats)
- Vendor claims I could not verify to a primary source: UGC-style creative has a 2.1x install-rate advantage; Spark Ads have 28% lower CPA than In-Feed — [Collab Only](https://collabonly.com/blog/posts/ugc-vs-professional-app-demo-video/); [LaunchShots](https://launchshots.app/blog/tiktok-ads-apps-2026)
- A funded-company case (Series B fintech, with paid spend): 1.1M views and 4,800 installs in month three, roughly 0.4% views-to-installs. Not comparable to an indie (vendor blog) — [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-growth-strategies-2026)
- An agency reports 4.0M organic views from 41 creator videos for an AI video app before the app was live, so no install data exists (agency case study) — [Agniverse](https://agniverse.co/media/case-studies/ai-video-app-4m-views-before-launch)
- Monetization context for any installs gained: RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115,000+ apps, mostly 2025 data) puts median download-to-paid at about 2.0% within 35 days overall and about 2.6% in North America; hard paywalls 10.7% versus freemium 2.1%. One year after launch, most apps are still small and only breakouts clear about $429 a month (secondary summaries of the report; regional figure varies between 2.6% and 2.8% by source) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/); [PPC Land summary](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic, not a measured rate: if views-to-installs is in the 0.01% to 0.5% range implied by the vendor examples above (40 per 500K at the bad end, 4,800 per 1.1M at the funded-company end), a reel with 300 views yields between 0 and 1.5 installs. Three reels a week at that reach means roughly 0 to 20 installs a month. The existing brand's reach is not, by itself, a distribution channel for an app.
- The existing audience's value is fit, not size. If the app serves the same Ethiopian/Eritrean diaspora audience that already watches the reels, conversion per view should sit toward the upper end; if the app is a general utility, the audience is the "wrong audience" case.
- The "account farm" and paid UGC-creator tactics are volume plays that need either money (creators) or time (many accounts); neither fits "limited time, small budget", and I found no independent evidence of their hit rate.
- Published success stories are survivorship-biased and mostly published by companies selling the tactic; failure is the base case and is rarely written up.

### Gaps
- No independent, documented conversion rate from organic TikTok/Reels/Shorts views to App Store installs.
- No verified indie case studies with full numbers (views, profile visits, link clicks, installs) in my searches; Reddit threads did not surface. RevenueCat, Superwall and Adapty blog posts on account farms or slideshow accounts were not found.
- No data on how often organic short-video app marketing fails, beyond anecdote.
- Nothing found on Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts specifically as install drivers; all material is TikTok-centric.

## 3. Other free channels: Reddit, Product Hunt, niche communities, churches and community organizations, SEO landing pages, Apple featuring, influencers on revenue share

### Takeaway
Documented results for Reddit and Product Hunt launches by solo developers are small (tens of downloads), and I found no data at all on churches, community organizations, SEO landing pages or revenue-share influencers. Apple featuring is the one free channel with an official, documented process: a Featuring Nomination in App Store Connect with at least two weeks' notice.

### Cited Findings
**Apple featuring (official)**
- Featuring Nominations in App Store Connect are the route to tell Apple's editorial team about an app launch, new content or an enhancement — [Apple, Getting Featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)
- Lead time: "please give our team a minimum of two weeks notice"; for wider consideration Apple recommends nominating up to three months ahead — [Apple, Getting Featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)
- Apple says there is "no checklist of requirements"; editors consider user experience, UI design, innovation, uniqueness ("a fresh approach to a familiar category"), accessibility, localization ("culturally relevant and appropriate content"), and the product page (screenshots, previews, description, ratings and reviews) — [Apple, Getting Featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)
- "All apps and games are eligible" for App of the Day whether new or old; In-App Events can surface on Today, Apps tabs, search results and the product page; featured developers get a notification in the App Store Connect app and may be asked for promotional artwork — [Apple, Getting Featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)
- WWDC 2026 added featuring nominations for game offers in the Apple Games app; games only — [AppTweak WWDC 2026 recap](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-wwdc-2026-recap)

**Product Hunt and Reddit (anecdote)**
- Scrollified (Indie Hackers, roughly early 2026): launched on the App Store, then Product Hunt a week later; ranked #38 on a day with 300+ launches; about 15% App Store conversion; 3 App Store reviews and 2 paying subscribers (search-snippet only) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-an-app-to-stop-infinite-scrolling-then-launched-it-on-product-hunt-38-here-s-what-actually-happened-50c73fc746)
- Headroom (Substack, date not confirmed, likely older): Product Hunt launch brought about 20 downloads; an r/macapps post got almost 6K views "but not many downloads"; the developer still valued Product Hunt because the page is well indexed by Google (search-snippet only) — [Chipp Studio Substack](https://chippstudio.substack.com/p/1-launch-producthunt-first-update)
- Older (2023): a #2 Product of the Day team credits pre-launch audience warm-up and replying to every comment — [The Big Bang Launch](https://thebigbanglaunch.substack.com/p/launchpedia-story)

### Inferences
- Product Hunt's audience is tech early adopters; for a diaspora-focused consumer app it is a backlink and credibility play, not an install channel.
- For this reader, the featuring criteria of "uniqueness" and "localization with culturally relevant content" are the best free angle: a well-made app serving Amharic or Tigrinya speakers is the kind of story editorial teams can use (for example around Ethiopian New Year in September or heritage moments), provided the product page and ratings are in order. This is my reading of Apple's criteria, not an Apple statement.
- Offline community channels (churches, community centres, restaurants, student associations) are undocumented in the literature but are the closest thing this reader has to an unfair advantage: high trust, concentrated audience, no algorithm. Treat any expected yield as unknown until tested.

### Gaps
- No data found on churches, mosques, community organizations or diaspora media as app distribution channels.
- No data found on SEO landing pages for small iOS apps (traffic or install conversion).
- No documented numbers on influencer revenue-share deals for small apps (typical splits, tooling, results).
- No aggregate Reddit data; individual subreddit self-promotion rules were not checked.
- No published acceptance rate for Featuring Nominations.

## 4. Apple Search Ads at tiny budgets ($5 to $20 a day)

### Takeaway
US median cost per install in 2025 was roughly $1.50 to $3.80 for the categories a small utility, lifestyle, education or health app would sit in, so $5 to $20 a day buys about 2 to 10 installs a day. Against a median download-to-paid rate near 2 to 2.6%, that implies roughly $100 to $190 per paying customer, which does not pay back for a low-price app; the defensible uses are brand-keyword defence, cheap niche keywords and learning which keywords convert.

### Cited Findings
- AppTweak US search-results benchmarks, January to December 2025, about 2,800 US advertisers, medians not weighted by spend (CPT / CPI / conversion rate / tap-through rate) — [AppTweak Apple Ads benchmarks 2026](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks?lang=en-US):
  - Health & Fitness: $1.68 / $3.77 / 50.3% / 7.5%
  - Lifestyle: $1.18 / $2.62 / 54.8% / 7.4%
  - Education: $1.52 / $2.91 / 56.4% / 8.3%
  - Productivity: $1.74 / $3.58 / 56.5% / 8.3%
  - Utilities: $1.23 / $2.25 / 63.2% / 10.6%
  - Reference: $1.14 / $2.09 / 57.5% / 9.8%
  - Books: $0.88 / $1.53 / 57.9% / 7.6%
  - Social Networking: $0.90 / $1.45 / 65.3% / 8.5%
  - Music: $1.14 / $1.92 / 61.4% / 9.0%
  - Entertainment: $2.42 / $4.99 / 57.9% / 7.6%
  - Business: $3.33 / $6.86 / 55.6% / 7.8%
  - Finance: $4.17 / $9.65 / 50.7% / 5.6%
  - Sports: $14.56 / $26.70 / 63.8% / 5.8%
  - Food & Drink: not in source
- SplitMetrics (global, 2025 data; sample 5M keywords, 6.3B impressions, 315.6M downloads, 91 markets): average CPA $3.76, average CPT $2.25 across the top 15 categories, TTR 9.7%, CR 66.2%; most expensive categories by CPA were Sports $19.74, Finance $13.28, Medical $8.90. North America has the highest CPT and CPA; September was the cost peak and April the cheapest window — [SplitMetrics blog, 2026-09-14](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/apple-search-ads-cost); [SplitMetrics 2026 report](https://splitmetrics.com/apple-ads-search-results-benchmarks-2026/executive-summary)
- Trend: SplitMetrics average CPA was $2.58 in 2023, $2.90 in 2024 and $3.76 in 2025 — [SplitMetrics 2025 CPA page](https://splitmetrics.com/apple-ads-search-results-benchmarks-2025/average-cost-per-acquisition-cpa/); [SplitMetrics blog](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/apple-search-ads-cost)
- Conflict to flag: an earlier AppTweak edition listed different US figures for some categories (for example Games $12.28 and Shopping $6.20 CPI versus $4.63 and $1.33 in the 2026 edition); use the 2026 edition and name the dataset — [AppTweak earlier edition](https://www.apptweak.com/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks?page=6)
- Apple added more ad placements within search results from March 2026 — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/18/app-store-search-results-more-ads-2026/)
- Payback inputs: median download-to-paid within 35 days about 2.0% overall and about 2.6% in North America; freemium 2.1%, hard paywall 10.7% — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)

### Inferences
- Installs per day at US medians: $5 a day buys about 1.3 (Health & Fitness) to 3.4 (Social) installs; $20 a day buys about 5 to 14. A $300 total test budget buys roughly 80 to 200 installs.
- Cost per paying user at a 2 to 2.6% download-to-paid rate: about $100 to $190 at $2.60 to $3.80 CPI. A $2.99 one-time app nets about $2.54 after Apple's 15%; it cannot pay back at any benchmark CPI. A $30 a year subscription nets about $25.50 in year one and also does not pay back at median conversion. Payback needs either a hard paywall with high conversion, a much higher price, or CPIs far below median.
- CPIs for ultra-niche keywords (Amharic, Tigrinya, Habesha terms) may be well below category medians because few advertisers bid, but volume will be tiny; the budget may simply not spend. Untested assumption.
- Benchmarks are medians from advertisers with established product pages and ratings; a new app with few ratings will likely convert worse than the 50 to 65% tap-to-install rates shown.
- The most valuable output of a $100 to $300 test is keyword-level conversion data to feed ASO, not the installs.

### Gaps
- No source on Apple Ads performance specifically at $5 to $20 a day, or on Apple Ads Basic versus Advanced for tiny budgets.
- SplitMetrics per-category US figures are gated; AppTweak has no Food & Drink row.
- No documented payback case studies for low-price indie apps on Apple Ads.

## 5. App Store Review: guidelines, the 2025 to 2026 crackdown, AI-built app rejection patterns, Apple statements on AI coding tools

### Takeaway
Apple has no rule against apps written with AI coding tools, ships agentic coding (Claude Agent and Codex) inside Xcode itself, and its one publicised "vibe coding crackdown" (March 2026) targeted apps that generate and run code on-device under 2.5.2, not apps authored with AI. The real exposure for an AI-built app is ordinary: 4.3(b) and 4.2 for anything that looks generic or thin (4.3(b) was tightened in June 2026), 2.1 completeness, 5.1 privacy (including the November 2025 third-party-AI consent rule) and 3.1.2 subscription disclosure.

### Cited Findings
**Apple and AI coding tools**
- Xcode 26.3 added agentic coding with Anthropic's Claude Agent and OpenAI's Codex: release candidate announced 2026-02-03, general release 2026-02-26. Agents install from Xcode settings, developers use their own Anthropic or OpenAI accounts, and Xcode exposes its tools over Model Context Protocol so other agents can connect — [9to5Mac, 2026-02-26](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/26/apple-releases-xcode-26-3-with-support-for-agentic-coding/); [InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/02/xcode-26-3-agentic-coding); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/02/04/apple-xcode-26-3-agentic-coding/)
- Apple's May 2026 fraud-prevention release says "powerful AI development tools drive a surge in app submissions" and does not mention any restriction on AI-built apps; it says App Review itself uses AI to "identify complex malicious patterns" and "analyze app similarity" — [Apple Newsroom, May 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/the-app-store-stopped-over-2-point-2-billion-usd-in-fraudulent-transactions-in-2025/)
- Greg Joswiak said rumors of the App Store's death in the AI age "may have been greatly exaggerated" — [TechCrunch, 2026-04-18](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/)

**The March 2026 "vibe coding" enforcement**
- The Information reported (around 2026-03-18) that Apple blocked updates to vibe-coding builder apps including Replit and Vibecode, citing guideline 2.5.2 (no downloading, installing or executing code that changes app functionality). An Apple spokesperson was paraphrased as saying the rule does not specifically target vibe-coding apps. No direct Apple quote was found — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/apple-reportedly-blocks-vibe-coding-apps-from-publishing-updates/); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2026/03/18/apple-blocks-ai-vibe-coding-apps/)
- Reported remedies: Replit to open generated apps in an external browser; Vibecode told updates would likely pass if it removed the ability to create apps for Apple devices. Vercel's v0 continued shipping updates — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/apple-reportedly-blocks-vibe-coding-apps-from-publishing-updates/)
- Apple removed the builder app "Anything" on 2026-03-26; it was reported back by early April. Replit's update reportedly returned after about four months (secondary sources) — [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/apple-blocks-updates-for-vibe-coding-apps-over-app-store-rules); [Gadget Hacks](https://apple.gadgethacks.com/news/replit-iphone-app-update-arrives-after-4-month-app-store-review-issue/)
- Current 2.5.2 text: apps "may not ... download, install, or execute code which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app, including other apps" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Guideline 4.3 Spam and 4.1 Copycats (current text)**
- 4.3(a): do not create multiple Bundle IDs of the same app; use one app with variations via in-app purchase — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 4.3(b), as tightened in June 2026: "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available. Opportunistically creating variants of existing app categories or popular apps degrades App Store discovery..." Named saturated categories: "dating, flashlight, sound effects, wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling", not accepted "unless they offer a meaningfully different or improved experience". "We may remove these apps from the App Store going forward if they are not updated, improved, or do not attract customers." Low-effort apps "do not add value"; repeated submissions "may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [9to5Mac, 2026-06-09](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/09/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines/)
- 4.1(c), new on 2025-11-13: "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." Relevant to any "replacement for X" naming or metadata — [Apple Developer News, 2025-11-13](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=ey6d8onl)
- Typical 4.3 rejection wording reported by developers: the app "shares a similar binary, metadata, and/or concept as apps submitted to the App Store by other developers, with only minor differences" (Apple forum thread and vendor blogs) — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812655)

**Guideline 4.2 Minimum Functionality**
- "Your app should include features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website. If your app is not particularly useful, unique, or 'app-like,' it doesn't belong on the App Store." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 4.2.6: "Apps created from a commercialized template or app generation service will be rejected unless they are submitted directly by the provider of the app's content." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Completeness and metadata (2.1, 2.3.1)**
- 2.1(a): final versions only; no placeholder text, empty websites or temporary content; working URLs; demo account if there is a login; back end switched on. 2.1(b): in-app purchases must be complete, visible to the reviewer and functional — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 2.3.1(a): new features must be described specifically in Notes for Review ("generic descriptions will be rejected"); misleading marketing is grounds for removal — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Privacy (5.1)**
- 5.1.1(i): a privacy policy link is required both in App Store Connect metadata and inside the app; it must state what is collected, how, all uses, third-party sharing, retention and deletion, and how to revoke consent — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(v): "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login. If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.2(i), revised 2025-11-13: "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." Apple does not define "third-party AI". Blog claims that the rule covers de-identified data or that a privacy-policy mention is insufficient are interpretation, not Apple text — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=ey6d8onl); [TechCrunch, 2025-11-13](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)
- 5.1.2(i) also: the app may not require users to enable push notifications, location or tracking to access functionality — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(ix): apps in highly regulated fields ("banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use, air travel and crypto exchanges") or that require sensitive user information "should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Health data (5.1.3, 1.4.1)**
- 5.1.3(i): health, fitness and HealthKit data may not be used for advertising, marketing or data mining; "You must disclose the specific health data that you are collecting from the device." 5.1.3(ii): no false or inaccurate data written to HealthKit, and apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 1.4.1: medical apps get greater scrutiny; accuracy claims for health measurements must disclose data and methodology; sensor-only blood pressure, temperature, glucose or oxygen claims are not permitted; apps "should remind users to check with a doctor" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**AI content and user-generated content**
- 1.2 explicitly includes "AI content creation apps": such apps need objectionable-content filtering, a report mechanism, user blocking and published contact information. June 2026 revision added that developers are responsible for removing violating content — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [9to5Mac, 2026-06-09](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/09/apple-tightens-app-review-guidelines/)
- 4.7 covers chatbots among "software not embedded in the binary"; the developer is responsible for all such content. Revised 2025-11-13 to add age-rating and age-restriction requirements (4.7.5) — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=ey6d8onl)
- There is no dedicated AI section in the guidelines — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Subscriptions and paywalls (3.1.2)**
- 3.1.2(a): auto-renewing subscriptions must provide ongoing value, last at least seven days and work across the user's devices; do not remove functionality existing users already paid for when moving to subscription; apps that "trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch" are removed and the developer may be expelled — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price" and communicate the Schedule 2 requirements — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple removed nearly 59,000 apps for bait-and-switch in 2025 — [Apple Newsroom, May 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/the-app-store-stopped-over-2-point-2-billion-usd-in-fraudulent-transactions-in-2025/)

**Rejection volume (documented)**
- 2025: over 9.1 million submissions reviewed; over 2 million rejected (1.2 million+ new apps, nearly 800,000 updates), about 22 to 23% of submissions. Over 371,000 rejected as spam, copycat or misleading; over 443,000 for privacy violations; over 22,000 for hidden or undocumented features; over 2.5 million TestFlight submissions blocked; 193,000 developer accounts terminated for fraud; 138,000+ developer enrollments rejected — [Apple Newsroom, May 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/the-app-store-stopped-over-2-point-2-billion-usd-in-fraudulent-transactions-in-2025/)
- 2024 comparison: over 320,000 submissions rejected as spam, copies or misleading — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/18/the-app-store-is-booming-again-and-ai-may-be-why/)
- Apple's 2025 Transparency Report by guideline section (figures read from a garbled page render; verify before quoting): Performance about 1,354,418, Legal about 495,673, Design about 415,532 (Design covers copycats, minimum functionality and spam), Business about 283,820, Safety about 151,159; 387,087 submissions approved after an earlier rejection — [Apple 2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/)

**Reported rejection patterns for AI-built apps (vendor blogs, unverified)**
- Vendor guides list 2.1 (crashes, placeholders, broken flows), 4.2 (thin or WebView-wrapper apps), 4.3 (template look, same concept as many others) and 5.1.1 (missing privacy policy, account deletion, permission strings) as the guidelines AI-built apps most often hit; also screenshots showing features absent from the build — [Greensighter](https://www.greensighter.com/blog/vibe-coded-app-app-store-rejection); [Axon Build](https://axonbuild.com/blog/vibe-coded-app-rejected-app-store/); [Applora](https://www.applora.io/guides/app-store-review-2026)
- One writer states they found no public 2026 case of an ordinary app rejected simply for being AI-generated, and that no rejection rate for AI-built apps is published — [pub-lish](https://www.pub-lish.com/en/read/9a156f11-1f21-4615-b6aa-596c233b7be9)
- Unverified vendor numbers to treat with caution: "spam rejections are about 28% of all rejections"; "90% of submissions reviewed within 48 hours" attributed to an Apple spokesperson — [Pixelbeard](https://www.pixelbeard.co/post/apple-is-rejecting-vibe-coded-apps); [Applora](https://www.applora.io/guides/app-store-review-2026)
- Former App Store review chief Phillip Shoemaker warned the submission surge could strain Apple's review resources (search-snippet only) — [TechSpot](https://techspot.com/news/113213-apple-app-store-inundated-low-quality-vibecoded-apps.html)

### Inferences
- A native SwiftUI app compiled into its own binary and submitted by its author is outside the 2.5.2 enforcement entirely; that action concerned apps that run generated code at runtime.
- The practical risk is that AI-built apps cluster in the same categories (utilities, lifestyle, productivity, health and fitness are the fastest-growing release categories) and resemble each other, which is exactly what 4.3(b) and Apple's AI-based "app similarity" analysis are aimed at. A workout tracker is a crowded category; a culturally specific app is naturally differentiated.
- The June 2026 sentence allowing removal of apps that "do not attract customers" means approval is no longer the end of 4.3(b) exposure for apps in saturated categories; low-traction apps can be culled later.
- If the app sends any user-entered data to an LLM API (Anthropic, OpenAI and so on), 5.1.2(i) points to an explicit in-app consent step naming the provider before the first call, plus matching privacy-policy and App Privacy label entries. That is the conservative reading; Apple has not published implementation specifics.
- For a fitness or health app: store nothing health-related in iCloud key-value or CloudKit as "personal health information", disclose each HealthKit type, make no measurement-accuracy claims. An individual account is acceptable for a workout log; 5.1.1(ix) would bite only if the app drifts into healthcare services.
- With about 1 in 4.4 submissions rejected store-wide, a first-submission rejection is common and usually curable; 387,087 submissions were approved after an earlier rejection in 2025.

### Gaps
- No Apple on-the-record statement about apps written with AI coding agents beyond the paraphrased spokesperson line and the Xcode announcements; no direct quote found.
- No published rejection rate for AI-built apps, and no verified r/iOSProgramming or developer-forum threads surfaced in my searches.
- Current App Review turnaround times in 2026 were not verified from Apple.
- The October 16, 2026 guideline revision (new 2.3.14, 2.5.19, rewritten 3.1.1(a) external link rules) was seen only in a search snippet; I did not read Apple's notice or the guideline text past section 5.2.5, so section 5.6 (Developer Code of Conduct) and any late-2026 changes are unverified.
- Schedule 2 subscription disclosure specifics (price, duration, terms and privacy links on the paywall) were not pulled from the agreement text.

## 6. Legal and practical basics for a US solo seller

### Takeaway
A US individual can sell on the App Store without an LLC or D-U-N-S number; the trade-off is that the seller name shown is the person's legal name. Apple collects and remits US sales tax on App Store sales, a US tax form in App Store Connect is mandatory for paid apps, and the Small Business Program (15% commission under $1M in proceeds) needs a one-time enrollment.

### Cited Findings
- Individual accounts display the developer's legal name as seller; a brand or DBA cannot replace it. Organization accounts display the legal entity name and require a D-U-N-S number (third-party guides, consistent with each other) — [Choicely](https://www.choicely.com/blog/individual-vs-organization-developer-account); [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/tech-news/apple-developer-account-requirements-for-individual-vs-business-developers)
- Converting an individual account to an organization later is possible; it requires being a founder of the entity and supplying its D-U-N-S number, and Apple may request business documents. D&B can take up to 5 business days to assign a number and Apple up to 2 more to receive it — [MobiLoud docs](https://mobiloud.com/docs/convert-individual-apple-developer-account-to-organizational)
- A developer reported that after converting to an organization with D-U-N-S verified, existing apps still showed the personal name as seller; resolution not found — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/813734)
- Guideline 5.1.1(ix) is the one place Apple pushes toward a legal entity: regulated fields (finance, healthcare, gambling, crypto exchanges and similar) or apps requiring sensitive user information — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Sales tax: Apple calculates, collects and remits US sales tax (and many VAT/GST regimes) on App Store purchases. One guide notes the phrase "merchant of record" does not appear in the Developer Program License Agreement, but the collection responsibility sits with Apple under its schedules. Developers still set tax categories in App Store Connect, and some state returns expect gross sales reported with marketplace sales backed out (third-party guides) — [Afternoon, US developer guide 2026](https://www.afternoon.co/blog/apple-app-store-sales-tax-guide); [Newly guide](https://newly.app/guides/who-actually-sells-your-app)
- A US tax form is "required for all developers" under the Paid Apps Agreement; App Store Connect provides tax documents and a monthly report of taxes Apple administered on US and Canadian sales — [Apple, tax forms overview](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/provide-tax-information/tax-forms-overview); [Apple, invoices and tax documents](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-tax-information/manage-invoices-and-other-tax-documents)
- Small Business Program: 15% commission on paid apps and in-app purchases for developers with up to $1M in proceeds in the prior calendar year, and for developers new to the App Store. Enrollment requires being the Account Holder, accepting the latest Paid Apps agreement and listing any Associated Developer Accounts. The reduced rate starts 15 days after the end of the fiscal month in which enrollment is approved. Exceeding $1M in the current year moves future sales to the standard rate — [Apple, Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- The same page states a further reduced 10% rate for subscriptions after their first year (and for developers on EU alternative terms), and that enrolled developers with fewer than 2 million first-time downloads can use Apple Foundation Models on Private Cloud Compute at no cloud API cost (requires the Private Cloud Compute entitlement). Both as read from the page via an automated summary; verify on the page before relying on them — [Apple, Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- Privacy policy: mandatory for every app, linked in App Store Connect and inside the app (see 5.1.1(i) above) — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Order of operations for a paid or subscription app: accept the Paid Apps Agreement, enter banking and the US tax form, then enroll in the Small Business Program before the first sale, because the 15% rate is not automatic and takes effect only after approval plus the fiscal-month lag.
- An LLC is a liability and branding choice, not an Apple requirement, for a workout log or cultural app. The cost of waiting is that the legal name appears on the store page and that conversion later involves D-U-N-S paperwork and a possibly sticky seller name.
- Income tax on proceeds remains the developer's responsibility (sole-proprietor Schedule C reporting in the US); this is general tax practice and was not sourced in this research.

### Gaps
- Did not confirm from an Apple page which US form an individual completes (W-9 is the expected form) or current payout thresholds.
- Washington State specifics (business licence, B&O tax treatment of marketplace-facilitated app sales) were not researched.
- Whether a US-only individual seller must declare EU trader status under the Digital Services Act when distributing in the EU was not researched (US-only distribution avoids the question).
- Current Apple Developer Program fee and any 2026 changes were not checked.
- The 10% subscription rate and the Private Cloud Compute benefit on the Small Business Program page need a direct read to confirm wording and effective dates.
